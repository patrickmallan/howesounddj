# Contact page streamline — selected date-first refinement V3

**Status:** Stage A–E refinement; production remains frozen.  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Patrick direction:** The current Contact page is disjointed, repetitive, confusing, visually flat, and contains language Patrick rejects. The intended journey remains date check first; if available, the visitor should be taken directly toward booking a consult.  
**Prior research:** V1 compared date-first and consult-first species. V2 removed the rejected Patrick photograph and studied pre-check and available-result states at desktop/mobile. V3 refines the selected date-first direction into a complete, shorter page composition with HSDJ artwork.

## One job

The page answers one question: **Is Patrick available for our wedding date?**

If yes, it immediately offers the consult. If the date cannot be confirmed automatically, Patrick checks it personally. If it is unavailable, the visitor can try another date or write. Someone without a date can send a message without reading a sales page first.

## Content decision

### Keep

- Contact label and Squamish / Sea-to-Sky context in the site chrome.
- Wedding date input and Check Availability action.
- Existing checking, available, unavailable, manual-review, edit-date, validation, accessible live-status, analytics, and Calendly behavior.
- Approved available-result headline: “This is the answer you were hoping for. Your wedding date is available.”
- The approved Stephen Henry excerpt and attribution.
- A plain message route for no-date, planner, venue, vendor, manual-review, or flexible-date enquiries.
- Direct email address as a low-key fallback.

### Remove from the page

- “Reach out when you are ready. I’ll make the next step clear.”
- The current pre-date Book a Consult feature section.
- Both three-button CTA groups.
- “What happens after you submit” and its four-step list.
- The “Helpful to include” container above the date checker.
- The always-expanded secondary inquiry form. Reveal it only when the visitor chooses the message route.
- “You do not need a perfect brief.”
- “Why couples reach out early” and “A clear path beats guessing in the final weeks.”
- The separate “Local to Squamish” sales card.
- The bottom “Ready when you are” finale.
- Repeated “No pressure,” “just clarity,” “right fit,” “clear path,” and “next step” language.

This removes six explanatory/sales sections and repeated action clusters. Contact becomes a functioning decision page rather than another long interior landing page.

Keep Stephen Henry’s exact governed excerpt below the action, not between the good news and the booking button.

These are draft directions, not replacements for the current governed copy.

## Reading path

Desktop: Contact → “Have a wedding date?” → date fields → Check my date → no-date message route → collage/proof.  
Mobile: header → art interruption → question → date fields → Check my date → message route.  
Available state: date confirmation → approved headline → consult explanation → Book a consult → Stephen’s review.

## Training-set gravity and verdicts

**Closest conventional ancestor:** contact hero with a lead form.  
**Regression risk:** adding a portrait, benefit bullets, three CTA cards, or a long trust section would turn it back into the rejected page.  
**Escape:** the date checker is the page, not a widget below marketing content; the artwork is structural edge material, not a card background.

**Anti-Design Auditor:** ADVANCE WITH CONDITION. Do not add more sections to make the page feel “complete.” The empty space is part of the decision.  
**Conversion Guardian:** ADVANCE WITH CONDITION. Preserve all result states, accessible status, validation, analytics, spam protection, and Calendly date context. On mobile, the available-state consult action must precede the testimonial.

**Sacrifice:** Visitors cannot book a consult before checking a date from the main path. A quiet direct scheduling route may remain in global navigation or be reconsidered with conversion evidence, but it may not become a competing hero button again.
