## Species A — Speaker Stack / Signal Cut

### 1. Species name

**Speaker Stack / Signal Cut**

### 2. Emotional job

Establish Patrick as the calm person operating inside real event energy. The visitor should feel the sound system's mass and the room's motion without being sold an underground-club persona.

### 3. Composition map

- **Desktop, 1440 × 1000:** documentary photograph fills the field; a narrow, full-height grille/label rail establishes equipment ancestry; a heavy signal block cuts horizontally through the photo; the headline occupies that block; the primary action protrudes beyond it like a backlit performance pad; the secondary action is physically attached below.
- **Mobile, 390 × 844:** the equipment rail remains full-height rather than becoming a header; photo and promise share one continuous field; the signal block rises into the image; the primary action projects to the right while the secondary action returns to the rail's baseline.

### 4–5. Static studies

- Desktop: `species-a-speaker-stack-desktop.png`
- Mobile: `species-a-speaker-stack-mobile.png`

### 6. Real HSDJ source used

`SRC-CH00-007 — Meghan & Brodie / Patrick at the booth`. Patrick, a real loudspeaker, booth, and camera-recorded light trails are all visible. Production permission and photographer attribution remain unresolved.

### 7. Physical ancestry record

- **Physical ancestor:** a loaded loudspeaker stack crossed by a live signal strip.
- **HSDJ source artifact:** the actual loudspeaker and booth in `SRC-CH00-007`.
- **Business meaning:** Patrick carries the technical weight so the wedding can feel effortless.
- **Transferred behavior:** vertical mass, protective grille, hard edge, illuminated action pad, signal interruption.
- **Transferred visual property:** dense black surface, perforation, abrupt yellow line, functional label orientation.
- **Properties deliberately not transferred:** knobs, meters, fake channel numbers, decorative cabling, simulated controls.
- **Why this could only belong to HSDJ:** the equipment language is anchored to Patrick's own working image and the copy frames him as a full-event operator, not a generic club DJ.

### 8. Type behavior

The promise is very wide, condensed, and forced into three blunt lines. It occupies the same physical strip as the action rather than sitting in a polite text column. Technical labels are small and subordinate. The mobile version maintains the vertical rail and cropped signal block. Font families are generic stand-ins used only to test width, compression, and hierarchy.

### 9. Reading path

- **First:** the real speaker, Patrick, and light movement.
- **Within ~2 seconds:** “packed dance floor” and “your wedding.”
- **Within 5 seconds:** Check Your Date, followed by HSDJ / Squamish.
- **Deeper inspection:** full-event support and the Sound Check consultation.

### 10. Intentional contradiction

Heavy professional sound equipment × Patrick's relaxed smile and warm wedding promise.

### 11. Training-set gravity test

- **Closest familiar web pattern:** full-bleed photographic hero with text overlay.
- **Evidence:** one dominant image and one high-contrast message block.
- **Regression risk:** the signal block could become a normal lower-third banner; black/yellow could become empty “DJ styling.”
- **Escape:** the full-height equipment rail, headline/action collision, physical protrusion, and image-sourced speaker mass control the page geometry.
- **Conventional usability retained:** the primary action is a high-contrast rectangle with direct wording; wedding, person, service, and location remain readable without knowing the metaphor.
- **Verdict:** `ADVANCE WITH CONDITIONS`.

### 12. Anti-Design Auditor verdict

The first draft was rejected because it remained a text-left/photo-right hero. This rebuild escapes that skeleton. Remaining concern: the grille pattern and yellow could still become costume if carried through the site without actual artifact evidence. Do not add decorative controls. **Verdict: ADVANCE WITH CONDITIONS.**

### 13. Conversion Guardian verdict

Wedding comprehension is supplied by the promise but not unmistakably by the crop alone. Patrick recognition and primary-action dominance are strong. Squamish is label-only and therefore weak. The mobile CTA is large and legible, but the vertical identity rail needs accessibility testing before implementation. **Verdict: ADVANCE; strengthen wedding and place evidence later.**

### 14. Sacrifice / tradeoff

Gives up airy luxury and immediate scenic Squamish romance in exchange for working credibility, energy, and equipment-derived physicality.

### 15. Patrick decision request

Does the real speaker mass feel like honest working authority, or does it make the arrival feel too equipment-heavy for a wedding? Should this species die, evolve, hybridize, or advance?

## Species B — Afterimage / Booth Flash

### 1. Species name

**Afterimage / Booth Flash**

### 2. Emotional job

Make the opening feel like a real remembered moment: imperfect, affectionate, energetic, and unmistakably human.

### 3. Composition map

- **Desktop:** huge words are cropped by the viewport like a pasted bill; a tilted vertical photograph cuts through them; the service promise arrives as a black interruption over the image; the action is a red strip colliding with the photograph rather than a button row.
- **Mobile:** the diagonal photographic slab consumes the upper field; fragments of the oversized words remain behind it; the promise becomes a black event-bill block attached to the photo's lower edge; the primary action is embedded inside it; the secondary action lives on the exposed paper below.

### 4–5. Static studies

- Desktop: `species-b-afterimage-desktop.png`
- Mobile: `species-b-afterimage-mobile.png`

### 6. Real HSDJ source used

`SRC-CH00-008 — Patrick & Hannah behind the controller`. The research derivative `source-patrick-hannah-oriented.jpg` only normalizes EXIF orientation; it is not a production asset. Production permission and depicted-person clearance remain unresolved.

### 7. Physical ancestry record

- **Physical ancestor:** an off-register flash print pasted over an oversized event bill.
- **HSDJ source artifact:** Patrick's informal blue-lit booth photograph with Hannah and the real controller.
- **Business meaning:** Patrick is part of the human celebration, not a vendor sealed behind equipment.
- **Transferred behavior:** crop, tilt, overlap, imperfect registration, abrupt ink blocks.
- **Transferred visual property:** blue event light, flash-era intimacy, warm paper, one urgent red action strip.
- **Properties deliberately not transferred:** fake distress textures, illegible punk typography, random tape, invented gig details.
- **Why this could only belong to HSDJ:** Patrick's expression, Hannah's presence, and the visible controller make the humanity and the job inseparable.

### 8. Type behavior

The background words are oversized beyond legibility and function first as rhythm. The service promise stays fully readable in a compressed black block. On mobile, the cropped word fragments remain atmospheric while all essential content resets to a clean left edge. Technical type is used only for true study labels and action hierarchy.

### 9. Reading path

- **First:** Patrick and Hannah in the blue booth moment.
- **Within ~2 seconds:** your people / your night, then the wedding promise.
- **Within 5 seconds:** Check Your Date and Howe Sound / Squamish.
- **Deeper inspection:** full-event support, consultation, and deliberate print/photo collision.

### 10. Intentional contradiction

An unpolished phone photograph × confident editorial scale and a precise conversion action.

### 11. Training-set gravity test

- **Closest familiar web pattern:** brutalist/editorial agency hero.
- **Evidence:** oversized cropped type, warm paper, red strip, and diagonal image.
- **Regression risk:** it could resemble fashionable anti-AI design rather than Patrick.
- **Escape:** the real imperfect HSDJ photograph is the governing object; the geometry follows its booth edge and portrait orientation, and the copy remains plainly wedding-specific.
- **Conventional usability retained:** the black promise panel and red CTA preserve legibility and action recognition.
- **Verdict:** `ADVANCE WITH CONDITIONS`.

### 12. Anti-Design Auditor verdict

This is the study most exposed to current editorial-brutalist fashion. The red strip and enormous type must never become a reusable style kit. Its defense is the actual human source and the refusal to fake distress. The desktop consultation line currently competes against the photograph and must be resolved in a next study. **Verdict: ADVANCE WITH CONDITIONS.**

### 13. Conversion Guardian verdict

Patrick recognition and emotional warmth are excellent. The service promise and date action are strong. “Wedding DJ” appears in the identity line, but the scene could be read as a party without that text. Squamish remains label-only. Mobile retains action prominence and avoids a generic card stack. **Verdict: ADVANCE; protect identity and copy legibility.**

### 14. Sacrifice / tradeoff

Gives up immediate composure and polish in exchange for intimacy, memorability, and proof that a real person is inside the work.

### 15. Patrick decision request

Is this imperfect blue booth photograph the right degree of raw and personal, or does the arrival need more wedding formality? Should this species die, evolve, hybridize, or advance?

## Species C — Load-In Docket

### 1. Species name

**Load-In Docket**

### 2. Emotional job

Communicate preparedness without corporate polish: the wedding is emotionally alive because Patrick quietly did the work before anyone noticed.

### 3. Composition map

- **Desktop:** one oversized working sheet fills the viewport; a tilted documentary print invades from the right; the promise is written like the event objective; the primary action is a black authorization block; a deliberately literal empty field identifies the missing HSDJ handwriting source.
- **Mobile:** the docket border remains a single physical object; the photo is clipped and taped into its upper-right; the objective sheet overlaps it; headline and action occupy a bordered instruction zone; the missing source field remains visible as evidence, not decoration.

### 4–5. Static studies

- Desktop: `species-c-load-in-docket-desktop.png`
- Mobile: `species-c-load-in-docket-mobile.png`

### 6. Real HSDJ source used / precise source requirement

- Used: `SRC-CH00-007 — Meghan & Brodie / Patrick at the booth`.
- Required: a real planning artifact made or marked by Patrick—handwritten set-list, reception-flow note, sound-check sheet, or venue load-in notation. It must show genuine pen pressure, abbreviation, crossing-out, and use wear. Capture flat or slightly oblique on a neutral surface; portrait or squarish orientation; no staged coffee, headphones, vinyl, or decorative props; private names/contact data redacted; Patrick need not be visible; equipment and Squamish need not be visible because the artifact itself must be HSDJ-authentic. The reserved field is lower-left on desktop and lower-third on mobile.

### 7. Physical ancestry record

- **Physical ancestor:** a marked-up sound-check/load-in document with a photograph physically attached.
- **HSDJ source artifact:** current photo is real; required handwritten/planning artifact is still missing.
- **Business meaning:** calm planning is the mechanism behind a full room.
- **Transferred behavior:** ruled boundary, event objective, annotation field, attached print, sign-off block.
- **Transferred visual property:** warm working paper, black rule, hard red correction, imperfect photo angle.
- **Properties deliberately not transferred:** fake coffee stains, random stamps, invented signatures, faux typewriter noise, bureaucratic clutter.
- **Why this could only belong to HSDJ:** it cannot meet that test until Patrick's real planning artifact replaces the literal source requirement.

### 8. Type behavior

The headline behaves as the largest instruction on the sheet, with one highlighted conclusion. Serif copy provides human contrast. Monospace is restricted to actual document metadata and source status. Mobile compresses the instruction zone but does not turn it into separate cards.

### 9. Reading path

- **First:** “event objective” and the packed-dance-floor promise.
- **Within ~2 seconds:** the real speaker/Patrick photograph and Check Your Date.
- **Within 5 seconds:** HSDJ / Squamish and Sound Check.
- **Deeper inspection:** planning artifact logic and the explicit missing-source requirement.

### 10. Intentional contradiction

Methodical pre-event paperwork × explosive light trails and a smiling operator.

### 11. Training-set gravity test

- **Closest familiar web pattern:** scrapbook/editorial portfolio.
- **Evidence:** paper ground, taped photograph, tilt, annotation field.
- **Regression risk:** maximal; fake tape and paper are established anti-AI clichés.
- **Escape:** every future mark must come from real HSDJ planning evidence; the placeholder announces missing truth instead of simulating it.
- **Conventional usability retained:** one dominant statement and one obvious action remain intact.
- **Verdict:** `REWORK PENDING SOURCE`.

### 12. Anti-Design Auditor verdict

The visual is promising but not yet earned. Without Patrick's actual paperwork it is a well-designed fiction and too close to fashionable collage. The study must not advance by inventing handwriting or distress. **Verdict: REWORK PENDING REAL ARTIFACT.**

### 13. Conversion Guardian verdict

Wedding outcome, Patrick, date action, and consultation are readable. Planning competence is strong. Squamish is only in the docket header. The paper density remains legible on mobile, but small document labels would not meet body-text accessibility requirements in production. **Verdict: HOLD until source and type-size problems are solved.**

### 14. Sacrifice / tradeoff

Gives up cinematic immersion and immediate emotional warmth in exchange for authored process, preparedness, and tactile working evidence.

### 15. Patrick decision request

Would you be willing to let a real planning note or set-list fragment become a defining opening artifact, or should paperwork remain a smaller supporting accent? Should this species die, evolve, hybridize, or advance?

## Species D — Runout / Loop

### 1. Species name

**Runout / Loop**

### 2. Emotional job

Frame the wedding as a unique live performance shaped around the couple's people, with a musical object that is instantly recognizable before any interface convention appears.

### 3. Composition map

- **Desktop:** the photograph becomes a huge record object that runs off the left edge; identity and event phases follow circular paths; a paper lyric/label sheet collides from the right; the primary action behaves like an inserted cue tab and sits outside the paper.
- **Mobile:** the record remains oversized and cropped; the paper sheet rises into it rather than stacking below cleanly; the action stays inside the sheet while the secondary action returns to the black runout field.

### 4–5. Static studies

- Desktop: `species-d-runout-desktop.png`
- Mobile: `species-d-runout-mobile.png`

### 6. Real HSDJ source used

`SRC-CH00-008 — Patrick & Hannah behind the controller`, with the same orientation-only research derivative used in Species B.

### 7. Physical ancestry record

- **Physical ancestor:** a record label, runout groove, and sleeve insert.
- **HSDJ source artifact:** Patrick and Hannah's booth photograph.
- **Business meaning:** each wedding is played live around the people actually in the room.
- **Transferred behavior:** looping path, repeated event sequence, off-centre spindle, sleeve/record collision.
- **Transferred visual property:** concentric scale, label core, hard paper insert, rotational crop.
- **Properties deliberately not transferred:** fake track time, catalogue numbers, decorative BPM, vinyl crackle, turntable controls.
- **Why this could only belong to HSDJ:** the label contains Patrick and a real wedding participant at his controller; however, the record device itself is broadly available to any music brand.

### 8. Type behavior

Identity and event phases follow arcs while the business promise remains straight and highly readable. Large condensed lines on the paper oppose the circle. On mobile the circle and sheet physically overlap, preserving the source tension. Curved type is contextual labeling, never essential reading.

### 9. Reading path

- **First:** Patrick and Hannah inside the oversized record.
- **Within ~2 seconds:** packed dance floor / your wedding.
- **Within 5 seconds:** Check Your Date, HSDJ, and Squamish.
- **Deeper inspection:** ceremony-to-last-song loop and Sound Check.

### 10. Intentional contradiction

A nostalgic fixed recording object × a promise that the wedding is shaped live in the room.

### 11. Training-set gravity test

- **Closest familiar web pattern:** music portfolio with a vinyl hero.
- **Evidence:** a literal circular record and label.
- **Regression risk:** high; vinyl is a common shorthand for “music,” and the object may overpower the wedding service.
- **Escape:** the circle is filled with Patrick's actual event evidence and is structurally interrupted by the conversion sheet.
- **Conventional usability retained:** essential copy and actions remain straight, large, and rectangular.
- **Verdict:** `REWORK`.

### 12. Anti-Design Auditor verdict

The composition is genuinely different, but the musical metaphor is too literal and broadly relabelable. It is at risk of being an elegant gimmick. The circular behavior would need a more HSDJ-specific source—perhaps an actual marked record/sleeve from Patrick's working history—to be earned. **Verdict: REWORK.**

### 13. Conversion Guardian verdict

Wedding and Patrick comprehension are strong; the date action remains dominant. Squamish is small and the Sound Check action is comparatively quiet. The desktop's action protrusion is memorable but would need careful focus order; curved labels cannot carry essential accessibility meaning. Mobile integrity is strong. **Verdict: USABLE STRUCTURE, but brand specificity is insufficient.**

### 14. Sacrifice / tradeoff

Gives up understatement and some immediate service clarity in exchange for a memorable musical object and non-linear reading behavior.

### 15. Patrick decision request

Does the record/runout logic feel naturally connected to your DJ life, or does it feel like a music-brand cliché? Should this species die, evolve, hybridize, or advance?
