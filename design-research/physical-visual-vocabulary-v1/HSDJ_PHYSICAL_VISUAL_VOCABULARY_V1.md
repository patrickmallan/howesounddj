## Outcome

The equipment folder contains a viable physical visual language. Its strongest material is not “DJ imagery” in the generic sense. It is the evidence of operation: button bezels, fader travel, centre marks, rotary ridges, printed legends, meter lights, worn surfaces, connector bodies and uneven LED spill.

The most credible design direction is therefore:

> Build the visual voice from photographed working controls and their surrounding evidence. Crop them like found fragments. Do not redraw them as polished symbols.

This is a vocabulary, not another CH 00 composition. No production file has been changed.

## Category grammar

### Buttons

Use for high-intent actions and interruptions: calls to action, state changes, navigation triggers and hard sectional punctuation. Retain the bezel, illumination, nearby labels and surface shadow.

### Faders

Use for progression, comparison, pacing and long directional movement. The rail, scale markings and cap position are essential; an isolated rectangle is not a fader.

### Rotaries

Use for circular counter-rhythm, local focus and controlled intensity. Centre marks, concentric machining and surrounding labels are the authentic features.

### Jog wheels

Use sparingly as large-scale anchors that can break a grid. Preserve reflections, edge depth and partial surrounding controls. Avoid record or vinyl clichés unless the actual object supports them.

### Meters, waveforms and LEDs

Use for signal, pace, motion and transitions. They are functional colour sources, not generic decoration. Never claim documentary set data without verified provenance.

### Labels, surfaces and wear

Use as the anti-template layer: printed legends, screws, brushed metal, rubber, dust, fingerprints and uneven falloff. These details should remain subordinate but visible.

### Cables, tape and cases

Use to show physical assembly and working reality. The current library lacks owned, provenance-safe material. Capture it; do not simulate it.

## Quality and extraction decision

All 18 extracted fragments are documentary rectangular crops. Processing is limited to orientation normalisation, cropping, proportional resizing and JPEG/PNG encoding. There is no generative fill, retouching, background replacement or invented hardware.

Transparent cutouts were not produced in V1. The strongest sources derive credibility from neighbouring surface, labels, shadows and wear; automatic isolation would remove that evidence, while generative masking risks changing real hardware edges. If isolated elements are required later, create them manually from a provenance-approved source and keep a visible surface margin.

## Missing objects and original assets recommended

1. Real gaffer tape: black, silver and fluorescent; torn, wrinkled, overlapped and marked by hand.
2. Patrick’s road cases: latches, corners, handles, foam, scratches, stickers and label plates.
3. Patrick’s working controls: crossfader, cue wear, meter columns, connections and cable paths.
4. Redacted real planning residue: set notes, timeline marks, crossed-out changes and pen pressure.
5. An original HSDJ material library: direct-flash macros of fingerprints, dust, powder-coated metal, brushed aluminium, rubber and cable jackets.

These should be photographed, not generated. Generated assets may be suitable later for abstract non-documentary light fields or transitions, but not for Patrick, weddings, Squamish, owned gear, real wear, tape, notes or historical evidence.
