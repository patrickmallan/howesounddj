# HSDJ Weddings Overview V2 — Mission Record

## One job

Answer one question quickly and convincingly: **What am I actually hiring Patrick to do across the wedding day?**

This is not the package page, the planning-process page, the review page, or another version of the homepage. It is the bridge between liking the brand and understanding the service.

## The decision this page should create

The visitor should leave thinking: “He has the music, microphones, timing, and room covered. I want to see what is included.”

The one page-level handoff is **See Packages**. Date checking remains a global utility in the header and the footer. The paired Book a Consult / Check Your Date mini stereo does not belong at the top or bottom of this page.

## Physical ancestor

A working wedding run sheet crossed with a DJ signal path:

- one line runs through the page rather than a collection of equal cards;
- cue marks and practical notes sit directly on the wedding imagery;
- ceremony, speeches, dinner, and dancing feel like one job changing shape;
- the composition looks handled, marked up, and used — not templated;
- DJ equipment language appears as controls, signal, and sequencing, not as a mixer photograph placed behind a paragraph.

## V2 visual elevation

The page now uses four original HSDJ editorial artworks created specifically for this overview. None of them is reused from the homepage or another interior page.

The visual tone is **techno flyer × punk paste-up × tasteful club light**:

- photographic reality remains visible, but it is interrupted by halftone, photocopy grain, torn paper, waveform traces, speaker mesh, control surfaces, and light movement;
- each wedding scene contains the sound work instead of sitting beside a decorative mixer photograph;
- people remain anonymous and secondary, avoiding fake documentary claims;
- cyan, acid yellow, warm amber, cobalt, green, and restrained red behave like signal colours rather than a carnival palette;
- irregular crops, dissolving dark edges, overlapping signal lines, and ink layers prevent the artwork from reading as photographs pasted into an album;
- Patrick’s retouched live-light image replaces the original top-right source image.

## Current page edit

### Cut

- duplicated Book a Consult / Check Your Date mini stereos;
- generic hero photography that does not sell or explain anything;
- “the room slowly lets go together” and other polished emotional-arc copy;
- repeating three-column benefit grids;
- the full planning process (it belongs elsewhere, if retained at all);
- package inclusions (Packages owns them);
- FAQ excerpts (FAQ owns them);
- multiple final calls to action.

### Keep, but rebuild

- the idea that the service covers the complete wedding day;
- real distinctions between ceremony sound, speeches, dinner, and the dance floor;
- useful explanations about playlists, dinner music, and requests;
- a clear onward path to Packages.

## Approved-copy candidate

### Opening

**WEDDINGS / WHAT YOU’RE ACTUALLY HIRING**

**YOU DON’T NEED TO BUILD THE WHOLE PLAYLIST.**

Tell me the songs you love, the ones you cannot stand, and the tracks already tied to the ceremony. I’ll build the rest and mix it live.

### The day / four different jobs

**CEREMONY**  
Exact track. Correct version. Cue it once.  
Processional songs, microphones, and timing are confirmed before anyone starts walking.

**SPEECHES**  
Before the mic goes live: coordinator ready, photographer ready, speaker ready, signal checked.

**DINNER**  
Upbeat doesn’t mean loud.  
Familiar records, room to talk, and no sleepy background playlist.

**DANCE FLOOR**  
Requests. Reactions. Volume. Next record.  
I watch what people stay for, what clears space, and what brings them back.

### Three useful truths

**FIVE HOURS OF SPOTIFY ISN’T TOO MUCH.**  
Most songs won’t play front to back. A long playlist tells me where your taste lives.

**DINNER MUSIC SHOULD NOT PUT EVERYBODY TO SLEEP.**  
I use upbeat classics and build the volume a little at a time.

**THE CURVEBALL CAN OPEN THE FLOOR.**  
It’s often the left-field rock song that makes heads turn and pulls people in.

### Handoff

**WANT THE DETAILS? SEE PACKAGES →**

## Desktop composition

The opening headline occupies the left two-thirds while a torn vertical strip of live DJ imagery intrudes from the right. The signal line begins at a cue marker beneath the opening, crosses four differently shaped wedding-day stages, then feeds three editorial notes that behave like a marked-up set list, record label, and tape strip. The page ends on a single oversized Packages handoff.

## Mobile composition

The same line becomes a vertical channel running down the left. Each stage attaches to that channel rather than collapsing into identical cards. Images and notes alternate across the available width. Headlines reduce deliberately instead of being broken into awkward single-word towers.

## Training-set gravity audit

- No interchangeable “premium service” cards.
- No icon + heading + paragraph grid.
- No vague promises about effortless, unforgettable, seamless, elevated, curated, or calm experiences.
- No repeated DJ-console photograph used as wallpaper.
- No invented emotional journey.
- No decorative section that lacks a selling or explanatory job.
- No CTA competition: one Packages handoff, with date checking held globally.

## Source integrity

The four wedding-stage images are original, generated HSDJ editorial artwork—not documentary photographs of HSDJ clients or named venues. They can illustrate the work, but the page must not claim that the depicted couples or locations are actual HSDJ events. Patrick’s retouched live-light image represents Patrick.
