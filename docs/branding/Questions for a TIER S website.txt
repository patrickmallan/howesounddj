The governing rule should be simple: every section must earn its place. It should communicate one useful idea, strengthen the Howe Sound DJ identity, and move the right couple closer to checking their date or booking a consult.

## Questions to ask of every section

### 1. What is this section’s single job?

Complete this sentence:

> “After seeing this section, the visitor should understand, feel, or do ______.”

If the answer contains several unrelated goals, the section needs to be divided or simplified.

### 2. What visitor question does it answer?

Examples:

- Will this DJ understand our music?
- Can he handle the ceremony and speeches properly?
- Will our dance floor actually feel alive?
- Does he know our venue and the Sea-to-Sky area?
- What makes him different from another wedding DJ?
- What happens after we inquire?
- Can we trust him with an important night?

If a section answers no meaningful question, it is likely decoration or filler.

### 3. Does it bring the visitor closer to booking?

A section should accomplish at least one of these:

- Create desire.
- Establish trust.
- Explain the service.
- Demonstrate expertise.
- Remove an objection.
- Provide proof.
- Make the next step feel easier.

A section that does none of these should be reworked, combined with another section, or removed.

### 4. Could another DJ company say the same thing?

This is the fastest test for generic copy.

Statements such as “We create unforgettable memories,” “Your day, your way,” or “Professional service from start to finish” could belong to almost anyone.

Howe Sound DJ copy should sound connected to:

- Patrick’s actual personality.
- Live mixing and reading the room.
- Wide-ranging musical knowledge.
- Squamish and Sea-to-Sky weddings.
- Proper sound and production experience.
- The difference between playing songs and building a dance floor.
- Couples who care about both their music and their guests.

### 5. Is it written like a DJ company—or like marketing software?

Read it aloud. Remove anything Patrick would never naturally say to a couple.

Watch for:

- Abstract language without a concrete meaning.
- Too many references to “flow,” “pacing,” “journeys,” “chapters” or “energy arcs.”
- Sentences that sound profound but communicate very little.
- Repeated promises using slightly different wording.
- Overly polished phrases that lack personality.
- Claims without an example, explanation or proof.

The language should be confident, musical, direct and human.

### 6. Is the strongest idea immediately visible?

A visitor should not need to read a paragraph to discover the point.

Check:

- Does the headline communicate something meaningful?
- Can it be understood without the supporting paragraph?
- Is the most valuable line visually dominant?
- Can someone skim the section and still understand it?
- Are important words being broken across lines?
- Is the supporting text short enough to invite reading?

### 7. Does the artwork reinforce the message?

DJ equipment should not merely decorate every section. Its purpose should be intentional.

Ask:

- Does the selected equipment relate to the subject?
- Does it help frame the text or compete with it?
- Is the text still effortless to read?
- Is this artwork meaningfully different from the previous section?
- Are we repeating the same mixer, card treatment or composition too often?
- Does the visual feel like Howe Sound DJ, or like a generic nightclub template?

The homepage establishes the creative vocabulary. Interior pages should speak that language without recreating the homepage composition.

### 8. Is there enough proof?

Important promises should be supported by something tangible:

- A real review.
- A named venue.
- A specific planning detail.
- Patrick’s audio-production background.
- A photograph from an actual wedding.
- A clear example of how he handles a situation.
- A concrete service inclusion.

Instead of saying “excellent communication,” show what that means: fast replies, clear next steps and no wondering who is handling what.

### 9. Is the next step appropriate?

Not every section needs a large booking button, but the visitor should never reach a dead end.

Possible next steps include:

- Check your date.
- Book a consult.
- See what is included.
- Read relevant reviews.
- Explore a specific venue.
- Understand packages.
- Hear more about Patrick.

Links should feel like the natural continuation of the section—not SEO links dropped into a paragraph.

### 10. Does it work on a phone?

Every section should be judged at mobile width, not merely checked after the desktop design is finished.

Verify:

- Headlines do not dominate the entire screen.
- Words are not chopped.
- Text does not collide with equipment.
- The reading order is obvious.
- Buttons remain easy to press.
- Decorative elements do not push the useful content too far down.
- The visitor can understand the section without excessive scrolling.

## Themes the entire website should reinforce

The site should repeatedly communicate these ideas without repeating identical sentences:

- Your music genuinely leads the night.
- Patrick mixes for the people in the room.
- Musical range is a strength, not an unfocused playlist.
- Strong preparation makes the wedding easier to enjoy.
- Professional audio matters during vows, speeches and dancing.
- Squamish knowledge is practical—not merely a local marketing claim.
- A wedding DJ can be polished without feeling formal or generic.
- The dance floor is built live, not prescribed in advance.
- Patrick is someone couples can talk to and trust.
- The experience is personal, skilled and distinctly different.

## Examine every page as a complete journey

Individual sections can be good while the overall page still fails. Each page should have a deliberate sequence:

1. **Orientation:** What page am I on, and is it relevant to me?
2. **Desire:** Why should I want this experience?
3. **Difference:** Why Howe Sound DJ?
4. **Understanding:** What do I receive and how does it work?
5. **Proof:** Why should I believe these claims?
6. **Objection removal:** What might stop me from inquiring?
7. **Action:** What is the clearest next step?

Not every page requires seven separate sections, but the page should cover the necessary stages without repeating itself.

## Practical review tests

Use these tests during every audit:

- **Five-second test:** After five seconds, can someone explain the section’s purpose?
- **Skim test:** Do the headlines alone tell a coherent story?
- **Delete test:** If the section disappeared, would the sales journey become weaker? If not, remove it.
- **Competitor-swap test:** Could another DJ replace the logo and keep all the words?
- **Read-aloud test:** Does it sound like Patrick speaking naturally?
- **Proof test:** What makes each important claim believable?
- **Objection test:** Which concern does this section resolve?
- **Visual competition test:** Are the artwork and words supporting each other?
- **Mobile-thumb test:** Can the section be understood and acted upon comfortably on a phone?
- **Repetition test:** Has this idea already been communicated more effectively elsewhere?

## Final keep, rework or remove decision

Every section should receive one outcome:

- **Keep:** It has one clear purpose, communicates it well and strengthens the booking journey.
- **Rework:** The purpose is valuable, but the writing, presentation, proof or next step is weak.
- **Combine:** It repeats another section and would be stronger as part of it.
- **Remove:** It provides no meaningful value or exists mainly to fill space.

This framework should become the editorial standard for the whole website. It prevents us from asking only, “Does this look impressive?” and forces the more important question: “Does this make the right couple understand, trust and want Howe Sound DJ?”

This document should become the website’s editorial constitution—not copy that appears on the site, and not merely a reference we occasionally remember.

Going forward, we should use it in five ways.

## 1. Before creating or redesigning a page

Every page should begin with a short page brief:

- Who is this page for?
- What brought them here?
- What is their main question or concern?
- What should they understand by the end?
- What is the page’s primary conversion action?
- What proof will make that action feel safe?
- What information belongs elsewhere?

This prevents us from filling pages with visually impressive sections that lack a clear business purpose.

## 2. Before creating every section

Each proposed section should receive a short “section contract”:

- **Single job:** After seeing this, the visitor should understand, feel or do what?
- **Visitor question:** What real question does it answer?
- **Business contribution:** Does it create desire, trust, understanding, proof or action?
- **Distinctive message:** Why could this only belong to Howe Sound DJ?
- **Evidence:** What makes the claim believable?
- **Next step:** Where should the visitor naturally go afterward?
- **Visual role:** What should the design help communicate?

If we cannot answer those questions clearly, we should not design the section yet.

## 3. As the acceptance test for completed work

A section is not finished because it looks polished. It is finished only when it passes four gates:

### Strategic gate

- It has one clear mission.
- It belongs on this particular page.
- It contributes to the booking journey.
- Removing it would make the page weaker.

### Writing gate

- Patrick could comfortably say it aloud.
- It avoids generic wedding-industry language.
- It does not overuse “flow,” “pacing,” “journey,” “energy arc” or similar filler.
- The headline communicates something meaningful on its own.
- The claim is concrete and preferably supported by proof.

### Design gate

- The visual treatment supports the message.
- DJ equipment is used intentionally rather than automatically.
- The section is visually distinct from its neighbours.
- Text remains more important than decoration.
- Nothing is clipped, overcrowded or unnecessarily difficult to read.

### Conversion gate

- The visitor understands what to do next.
- Links are contextually useful rather than inserted for SEO.
- The section removes friction instead of adding more decisions.
- The next action matches the visitor’s readiness.

## 4. During whole-page reviews

We should audit pages in two passes.

The first pass examines only the headlines. If the headlines do not tell a clear, persuasive story by themselves, the page structure is not ready.

The second pass examines the complete visitor journey:

1. Where am I?
2. Is this relevant to my wedding?
3. Why should I want this?
4. Why Patrick?
5. What exactly am I receiving?
6. What proves he can deliver it?
7. What concern might still stop me?
8. What should I do next?

This is important because ten individually attractive sections can still produce a confusing or repetitive page.

## 5. During sitewide audits

We should maintain a simple decision record for every section:

- Keep
- Rework
- Combine
- Remove

Each decision should include a one-sentence reason. This gives us a strategic map of the website and prevents removed filler from quietly returning in another form.

## How it should change our actual workflow

For future website work, the order should be:

1. Define the page’s audience, mission and primary action.
2. Inventory its existing sections.
3. assign each section a single job.
4. Remove or combine sections that repeat one another.
5. Rewrite the content in Patrick’s natural voice.
6. Match each section with purposeful imagery and equipment.
7. Build the desktop and mobile presentation together.
8. Perform the skim, read-aloud, proof, competitor-swap and delete tests.
9. Verify that the page ends with a logical conversion path.
10. Review the finished page against the document before calling it complete.

## What the document should not cause

It should not result in every page using the same formula, artwork or number of sections. The questions stay consistent; the answers should change according to the page.

For example:

- The Weddings page should establish fit, explain the experience and reduce uncertainty.
- The Reviews page should primarily provide proof.
- The About page should create personal connection and professional confidence.
- The Venues pages should demonstrate useful local knowledge.
- The Packages page should make comparison and decision-making easier.
- The Contact page should reduce friction and make taking action feel straightforward.

## Making it persistent

At the moment, the document exists in the branding folder, but future development work will not automatically follow it. The strongest next step is to convert its essential rules into:

- A persistent project rule that every future website task must follow.
- A reusable section-audit template.
- A short Howe Sound DJ voice and banned-language checklist.

The original document would remain the full source of truth, while those smaller tools would make it operational during everyday development.
