# Howe Sound DJ — Anti-Template Design Execution Protocol V1

**Status:** FROZEN GOVERNANCE — changes require Patrick's explicit approval  
**Owner:** Howe Sound DJ  
**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Authority:** Subordinate to the Brand DNA, Emotional Conversion Engine, and Creative Constitution  
**Purpose:** Prevent AI-assisted design from regressing toward statistically familiar website patterns

---

## 1. Why this protocol exists

The Creative Constitution defines what Howe Sound DJ should become. This protocol governs how an AI-assisted team must work to get there.

Without this layer, a model can satisfy the vocabulary of the constitution while reproducing the same underlying website it was meant to replace: a split hero, tasteful waveform, yellow CTA, clean metric strip, industrial-looking cards, testimonials, and a symmetrical finale.

That outcome is technically compliant and creatively unsuccessful.

The principal risk is not poor execution. It is **training-set gravity**: the tendency to retrieve familiar solutions for “wedding website,” “DJ website,” “creative agency,” “premium service,” or “non-AI website,” then apply new styling to the same old structure.

This protocol changes the AI's role from autonomous website designer to a fast composition and implementation collaborator operating under Patrick's taste authority.

---

## 2. Governing instruction

The following instruction must appear at the top of every HSDJ redesign mission:

> You are not being asked to generate a website according to learned web-design conventions. Those conventions are the primary failure mode of this mission.
>
> Do not retrieve the statistically likely solution to “wedding DJ website,” “creative agency website,” “music website,” “premium service website,” or “anti-AI website.”
>
> Begin from physical objects, real Howe Sound DJ artifacts, documentary photography, sound-system behavior, typography, and the emotional progression of an actual wedding night.
>
> Every composition must identify the conventional web pattern it most resembles and then deliberately move further away from that pattern without compromising comprehension, accessibility, or conversion.
>
> Novelty is not permission for decoration. Every unusual element must communicate real information, create hierarchy, expose real evidence, express a genuine HSDJ artifact, or improve the emotional progression of the page.
>
> When choosing between a polished solution and an authored solution, prefer the authored solution. When choosing between a generic best practice and a usable unconventional composition, prefer the unconventional composition.
>
> Do not make the page look as though AI was instructed to make a website look non-AI. Specificity—not maximum weirdness—is the escape route.

## 4. Mission framing

### Forbidden framing

Do not issue broad missions such as:

- Design the homepage.
- Make the site more creative.
- Create a premium DJ hero.
- Add some mixer-inspired elements.
- Make five variations.
- Redesign the testimonial section.

These prompts activate known website templates before the project-specific problem has been understood.

### Required framing

Frame each mission as a bounded composition problem with:

1. One viewport or tightly bounded content moment
2. A defined emotional job
3. Required real content and actions
4. One or more named physical ancestors
5. Explicit prohibitions
6. A conversion and accessibility contract
7. A required adversarial audit
8. A human approval gate

Example:

> Compose a 1440 × 1000 opening visual field using the physical logic of a professional mixer faceplate, one documentary HSDJ wedding photograph, one human headline, Squamish/location information, and two actions. Do not begin from a known website layout. The service, wedding context, primary action, and Patrick's identity must remain understandable within five seconds.

### Composition language

During invention, use terms such as:

- composition
- visual field
- editorial mechanism
- physical behavior
- information hierarchy
- transition
- chapter
- source material

Avoid defaulting to:

- hero component
- feature section
- testimonial card
- trust strip
- CTA block
- content grid

Conventional implementation names may return after a composition is approved, but they may not determine its form.

## 5. Default design prohibition list

The following are forbidden without explicit, recorded authorization from Patrick:

- Centered hero composition
- Conventional text-left / image-right hero
- Conventional image-left / text-right alternation
- Three-column feature grids
- Evenly sized content boxes
- Collections of cards
- Pill navigation
- Floating glass panels
- Glassmorphism
- Gradient backgrounds
- Gradient typography
- Ornamental blur or glow
- Repeated large border radii
- Generic outline icon sets
- Symmetrical feature sections
- Testimonials inside quote cards
- Conventional testimonial carousels
- Logos in a horizontal trust strip
- Stock or decorative waveform SVGs
- Fake mixer panels
- Equal-width navigation items used for neatness
- Generic sticky CTA bars
- Giant number plus small-label metric blocks
- Decorative `01 / 02 / 03` numbering
- Arbitrary monospaced type
- Arbitrary coordinates or technical data
- Decorative knobs, pads, meters, sliders, or switches
- Animation whose only purpose is “premium feel”
- Repeated scroll-reveal movement on every section
- Uniformly distressed textures
- A dark/yellow/monospace/brutalist treatment used as proof of originality

Absence from this list is not approval. Every proposal remains subject to training-set gravity review.

### Exception procedure

A prohibited pattern may be considered only when all four conditions are met:

1. It is clearly the most usable solution to a real problem.
2. The designer identifies why an unconventional alternative failed.
3. The Anti-Design Auditor confirms it is not being chosen for implementation convenience.
4. Patrick explicitly approves the exception.

The exception must be documented in the mission record.

Acceptable ancestors include:

- Rane or Pioneer performance pads
- Technics SL-1200 controls
- A record sleeve, label, or runout groove
- A real HSDJ flight case
- Gaffer-tape labeling
- Serato cue markers or a real waveform
- A venue stage plot
- A sound-check sheet

- Highway or industrial signage
- A real club handbill or event bill
- A risograph or photocopied poster
- An analog VU meter
- An equipment serial/specification plate
- A rack-mounted processor
- A crossfader or channel fader
- A venue load-in map

“Brutalist website,” “music website,” and “creative agency site” are not physical ancestors.

### Ancestry record

Each component proposal must declare:

```text

HSDJ source artifact, if available:
Business meaning:
Transferred behavior:
Transferred visual property:
Properties deliberately not transferred:
Why this could only belong to Howe Sound DJ:
```

Example:

```text

HSDJ source artifact: Patrick's photographed controller
Business meaning: Check wedding date
Transferred behavior: depression, illumination, active/disabled state
Transferred visual property: labeling rhythm and shallow physical depth
Properties deliberately not transferred: MIDI jargon and fake pad bank
Why this could only belong to HSDJ: the label, wear, colour, and interaction
come from Patrick's actual equipment and lead into his real availability flow
```

Physical borrowing without transferred meaning is cosplay and must be rejected.

## 7. Artifact-before-invention law

AI may not invent texture when a real Howe Sound DJ artifact can provide it.

Before proposing artwork, inspect the approved source library for:

- Patrick's handwriting
- Actual controller, mixer, case, tape, cable, label, and sticker details
- Real set lists and preparation notes
- Old HSDJ flyers, business cards, and Wix-era fragments
- Real photographs from weddings, load-ins, sound checks, and venues
- Playlist or crate material approved for public use
- Real Sea-to-Sky roads, weather, mountains, venues, and surfaces
- Customer language and Patrick's natural phrases

### Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Real HSDJ artifact
2. New photograph or scan made specifically for HSDJ
3. Transformation of an HSDJ artifact
4. Licensed external reference with a documented purpose
5. Newly invented artwork only when no authentic source can perform the job

Generated assets may assist ideation or create non-representational support material. They may not fabricate couples, weddings, Patrick's history, a venue, a piece of owned equipment, or documentary evidence.

### Traceability

Every incorporated artifact must record:

- Source and owner
- Date added
- Permission status
- Private information removed
- Transformations performed
- Pages/components where it is used

## 8. Separation of invention and implementation

Implementation pressure is not allowed to select the concept.

Every mission passes through the following stages in order.

### Pre-code repository freeze

During Stages A–E:

- No existing production source file may be modified.
- No production React component, route, stylesheet, design token, asset pipeline, dependency, configuration, analytics code, or functionality may be created or changed.
- No exploratory work may be smuggled into a production directory as a “temporary prototype.”
- Exploratory artifacts may exist only inside the mission's designated redesign research/prototype area.
- Existing production files may be inspected read-only for content, functionality, measurements, and constraints.
- Repository implementation begins only after the mission record contains the exact marker `PATRICK_CREATIVE_APPROVAL: GRANTED`.

If the marker is absent, ambiguous, or applies to another viewport, production mutation is prohibited.

### Stage A — Composition

No production code.

Produce structurally distinct composition maps and low-fidelity static visual studies in the designated research/prototype area. Establish hierarchy, source material, ancestry, type behavior, mobile translation, and the emotional job.

Written concept descriptions alone do not satisfy Stage A. Production React, route, or stylesheet prototypes violate the pre-code freeze.

### Stage B — Creative interrogation

For every composition, answer:

- What familiar web pattern is this unconsciously reproducing?
- What could belong to another DJ or another city?
- What looks “web designed” rather than authored?
- Where has symmetry or equal sizing crept in?
- Which element exists only because websites usually contain one?
- Which DJ reference communicates nothing?
- What feels like the emerging AI “anti-AI” aesthetic?

### Stage C — Destruction

Remove, interrupt, enlarge, crop, reorder, or recompose the conventional ancestry identified in Stage B. Do not merely add texture.

### Stage D — Usability reconciliation

Restore or confirm:

- Five-second service comprehension
- Wedding and Squamish context
- Clear reading order
- Check-date prominence
- Consultation clarity
- Keyboard and touch usability
- Contrast and legibility
- Responsive integrity

Recover clarity without restoring the rejected template.

### Stage E — Human taste gate

Patrick selects, rejects, or directs the degree of tension. No code begins without explicit approval of the composition.

### Stage F — Implementation

Translate the approved composition faithfully. Do not “clean it up” into easier reusable cards, standard sections, or symmetrical responsive patterns.

### Stage G — Certification

Run design fidelity, conversion, accessibility, responsive, performance, SEO, analytics, and functional checks before expansion.

## 9. Structurally different compositions

When multiple concepts are requested, they must be different structural species—not visual variants sharing one layout skeleton.

Possible opening-composition species include:

- **Mixer faceplate:** One continuous equipment surface; information behaves as controls and labeling.
- **Gig bill:** Typography and documentary photography dominate; technical fragments are pasted or printed into the hierarchy.
- **Record object:** Content emerges from the physical logic of sleeve, label, edge, rotation, and reveal.
- **Sound-check document:** A working technical sheet gradually breaks into emotional photography.
- **Waveform timeline:** The viewport begins one continuous temporal track through the night.

### Diversity requirements

- No two concepts may share the same underlying DOM/layout skeleton.
- Moving an image, changing a typeface, or changing the physical metaphor does not make a new structure.
- Each concept must use a different reading path, spatial hierarchy, and relationship between image, type, and action.
- Each concept must document what it sacrifices as well as what it gains.
- Hybridization happens only after the strongest structural idea is selected.

## 10. Training-set gravity test

Every proposal must identify its closest conventional ancestor before approval.

### Required statement

```text
Closest familiar web pattern:
Evidence of that pattern in this composition:
Risk of regression during responsive implementation:
What was changed to escape it:
What remains conventional for usability, and why:
Verdict: REJECT / REWORK / ADVANCE
```

### Examples

- “This is structurally a split hero with decorative mixer controls.” → **Reject**
- “This is a testimonial carousel wearing record-sleeve styling.” → **Reject**
- “This is a metrics strip relabeled as an equipment plate.” → **Rework unless the plate creates a genuinely different hierarchy or behavior**
- “This uses a familiar button because the action must remain obvious, but the surrounding composition has no hero/card ancestry.” → **Potentially advance**

Never ask merely, “Is this creative?” The designer is required to expose its own conventional inheritance.

---

## 11. Controlled incompatibility

AI-generated design often looks generated because everything harmonizes. Each major viewport must contain at least one intentional contradiction.

Examples:

- Beautiful wedding portrait × ugly technical serial plate
- Elegant display type × mechanical fader
- Warm candid image × cold industrial rule system
- Chaotic dance floor × extremely restrained copy
- Warm paper × brutal black equipment surface
- Precise alignment × one visibly human annotation
- Quiet planning evidence × abrupt peak-energy photography

The contradiction must support the emotional or informational purpose of the viewport. Random collision is not authorship.

### Reconciliation rule

Do not smooth the contradiction away during implementation. Instead, control it through scale, rhythm, contrast, and reading order.

## 12. Three functional authorities

Every composition must be evaluated through three separate roles. One person or model may perform the roles sequentially, but their outputs may not be blended into one vague self-review.

### DESIGNER

Responsible for:

- Structural invention
- Physical ancestry
- Emotional progression
- Artifact use
- Typography, composition, and visual tension
- Multiple genuinely different species when requested

The Designer cannot approve its own work.

### ANTI-DESIGN AUDITOR

Has authority to reject for:

- AI aesthetic
- Template ancestry
- Generic agency or wedding-industry language
- Unnecessary polish
- Symmetry and equalized blocks
- Decorative metaphor
- Faux-industrial styling
- Arbitrary technical labels
- Gimmicks
- Implementation-driven compromise
- Dependence on a written rationale to seem original

The Auditor must name the specific pattern, not say merely that a concept “feels generic.”

### CONVERSION GUARDIAN

Has authority to reject for:

- Unclear service or wedding context
- Hidden or weakened date checking
- Confusing consultation flow
- Loss of Patrick's prepared, capable presence
- Accessibility failure
- Mobile failure
- SEO or content loss
- Performance risk
- Novel interactions obscuring practical information

The Conversion Guardian cannot force the Designer back into a prohibited template when another usable solution is possible.

### Decision logic

```text
Designer invents
    ↓
Auditor attacks conventionality
    ↓
Designer reconstructs
    ↓
Conversion Guardian protects comprehension
    ↓
Patrick exercises taste authority
```

## 13. Visual proof outranks creative explanation

A concept that requires a paragraph explaining why it is unconventional has failed.

The following are not acceptable defenses:

- “The rounded rectangle evokes analog softness.”
- “The gradient represents the transition from ceremony to dancing.”
- “The cards resemble channels on a mixer.”
- “The monospace font gives a technical feeling.”

The question is not whether a choice can be rationalized. The question is whether the composition is visibly authored, useful, specific, and recognizably HSDJ.

Written rationale may document source and meaning. It may not rescue a generic visual result.

## 14. One viewport at a time

Do not redesign the entire homepage in one mission.

### Initial approval gates

1. `CH 00 — Arrival`, desktop at approximately 1440 × 1000
2. `CH 00 — Arrival`, mobile at approximately 390 × 844
3. Responsive relationship between those compositions
4. `CH 01 — Sound Check` only after CH 00 approval
5. One subsequent chapter at a time

Desktop and mobile must be related compositions, not unrelated artboards and not a desktop layout simply stacked vertically.

### Stop rule

At the end of each viewport mission:

- Stop.
- Present the work.
- Run the Auditor and Guardian reviews.
- Record unresolved tensions.
- Wait for Patrick's creative approval.

Do not use approval of one viewport as permission to redesign the remaining homepage or site.

## 15. Human taste authority

AI proposes. Patrick curates.

The following decisions remain explicitly human:

- Final typography selection
- Degree of ugliness or industrial tension
- Photographic crop and emotional image choice
- Amount of overlap and collision
- Which hardware references feel authentic
- Which artifacts feel personal enough to use
- Whether an element feels too designed
- Whether a musical reference is genuinely Patrick
- The boundary between expressive and gimmicky
- Final concept selection

The system must not average Patrick's strong reaction into a safer compromise. A clear rejection is creative data.

## 16. Mission deliverable contract

### Approved Stage A artifact format

For each composition species, Stage A must produce:

1. **Composition map** — a 1440 × 1000 spatial structure with approximate regions, scale relationships, collisions, crop, and hierarchy.
2. **Low-fidelity static visual study** — enough visible form to judge tension, density, proportion, reading order, and whether the composition is actually different.
3. **Real HSDJ image selection** — a named repository asset or newly supplied approved artifact; never “imagine a photograph here.”
4. **Type behavior study** — approximate scale, condensation, collision, line behavior, and technical/human hierarchy without pretending an unapproved font is final.
5. **Physical ancestry record** — completed in the required protocol format.
6. **Reading path** — the intended first five seconds followed by the deeper scan.
7. **Training-set gravity audit** — closest conventional ancestor, regression risk, escape, and verdict.
8. **Mobile translation** — a corresponding 390 × 844 composition that retains the same structural idea without becoming a vertical card stack.
9. **Anti-Design Auditor verdict** — specific pattern findings and `REJECT`, `REWORK`, or `ADVANCE`.
10. **Conversion Guardian verdict** — service, wedding, location, action, accessibility, and mobile findings.
11. **Sacrifice/tradeoff** — what the species intentionally gives up and why.
12. **Patrick decision request** — the exact taste decisions required before progression.

The composition map and static study may be raster, vector, or another fixed visual format, but they may not be implemented as production website components. They must be directly viewable and comparable at the target desktop and mobile proportions.

### Complete pre-code deliverable

Every pre-code composition mission must also provide the mission-level context:

1. Mission and emotional job
2. Required information and actions
3. Real source artifacts used or still needed
4. Physical ancestry record
5. Composition description or static study
6. Reading path
7. Intentional contradiction
8. Conventional pattern prohibition compliance
9. Training-set gravity test
10. Anti-Design Auditor verdict
11. Conversion Guardian verdict
12. Desktop/mobile relationship
13. Risks and sacrifices
14. Explicit decisions required from Patrick

No written essay or production component should be presented as a substitute for the required visible composition artifacts.

### Implementation handoff requirements

After approval, the handoff must additionally specify:

- Approved visual reference
- Elements that must not be normalized
- Responsive behavior
- Interaction states
- Reduced-motion behavior
- Keyboard/touch behavior
- Image sources and crops
- Typography and loading requirements
- Performance budget
- Existing functionality that must remain untouched
- Acceptance screenshots and test states

## 17. Rejection conditions

Reject a composition immediately when any of the following is true:

- It is a familiar website layout with DJ styling applied.
- Its originality depends mainly on grain, monospace, yellow, or oversized type.
- The physical metaphor communicates no real information.
- It could belong to another DJ after changing the logo.
- It could belong to a SaaS company after changing labels.
- It uses generic generated artwork where a real HSDJ artifact could exist.
- It makes Patrick look chaotic, aggressive, or musically inflexible.
- It obscures weddings, Squamish, availability, or consultation.
- Mobile collapses into an ordinary vertical card stack.
- Reduced-motion mode loses essential content.
- The unusual parts are difficult to use and the usable parts are generic.
- The design explanation is more distinctive than the design.

## 18. Certification questions

Before Patrick sees a composition, the team must answer:

### Specificity

- What exact HSDJ evidence is present?
- What exact Squamish or Sea-to-Sky evidence is present?
- What could not have been produced for another DJ?

### Structure

- What known web pattern was avoided?
- What remains conventional and why is it necessary?
- Does the mobile form retain the same authorship?

### Meaning

- What does each hardware reference communicate?
- What real information would be lost if the unusual element were removed?
- Is any element pretending to be interactive?

### Conversion

- Can a couple identify service, place, and next step in five seconds?
- Is check-date still dominant?
- Does Patrick feel competent, caring, and composed?

### Restraint

- Is there one strong visual idea or several competing tricks?
- Was conventionality mistaken for restraint?
- Has any tension been polished out during implementation?

### Integrity

- Are every review, metric, image, and availability claim real and governed?
- Are accessibility and performance part of the design rather than cleanup work?
