# Howe Sound DJ Website Redesign — Creative Constitution V1

**Status:** FROZEN GOVERNANCE — changes require Patrick's explicit approval  
**Owner:** Howe Sound DJ  
**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Applies to:** Website art direction, interface design, motion, photography, copy, and component decisions  
**Upstream authority:** `HOWE_SOUND_DJ_BRAND_DNA_REPORT_V1.md` and `HOWE_SOUND_DJ_EMOTIONAL_CONVERSION_ENGINE_V1.md`

---

## 1. The mandate

Build the website a DJ would make if nobody had ever told them what a wedding-DJ website was supposed to look like.

Howe Sound DJ must no longer resemble a polished wedding template, a luxury-service landing page, or a component library with DJ copy placed inside it. The website should feel authored by Patrick and rooted in Squamish, physical DJ equipment, record culture, real weddings, and the changing energy of a night.

The redesign is not permission to become confusing, juvenile, or theatrical. Couples must still understand within seconds:

1. Patrick is an experienced wedding DJ based in Squamish.
2. He handles the whole wedding calmly and professionally.
3. He can create an exceptional, packed dance floor without imposing his taste on the couple.
4. They can check their date or book a conversation immediately.

Everything else may become more expressive.

### Creative thesis

**THE WEBSITE IS THE DECK.**

Do not decorate a conventional website with DJ imagery. Derive the interface itself from the logic, materials, movement, and labeling of professional DJ equipment.

### Cultural thesis

**SEA-TO-SKY SOUND SYSTEM.**

DJ hardware alone would create generic nightlife branding. The proprietary identity comes from colliding sound-system culture with Squamish: mountains, weather, wood, concrete, black water, highway markings, venue exteriors, outdoor ceremonies, cables, speakers, and crowded dance floors.

### Professionalism discipline

Professionalism, reliability, and wedding competence must be demonstrated through clarity, follow-through, legibility, trustworthy actions, and real evidence. They must not be used to soften the visual system or repeatedly verbalize reassurance. Patrick may appear fully inside the energy of the room without being recast as an invented underground-club persona.

## 2. The brand truth beneath the redesign

The design may change radically. The customer-proven brand must not.

Existing reviews consistently support these truths:

- Patrick is composed, professional, personable, and caring.
- Planning feels seamless and stress-reduced.
- He supports the full event, not only the dance portion.
- The music is mixed live with strong transitions and no dead air.
- Dance floors stay packed and energy stays high.
- Couples see him as part of their team and recommend him decisively.

The redesign must give these truths a more ownable form. It must not replace them with an invented underground-DJ persona.

### Required tension

The brand is strongest when two qualities appear together:

| Side A | Side B |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared control | Physical release |
| Wedding competence | Club-level musical fluency |
| Local familiarity | Wide musical range |
| Professional equipment | Human warmth |
| Beautiful photography | Industrial interface |
| Clear decisions | Designed disorder |

Any concept that contains only one side is incomplete.

## 3. The instant-comprehension contract

Expression is allowed around the conversion path, not in place of it.

### Above-the-fold requirements

The first viewport must contain:

- `Howe Sound DJ` or `Howe Sound Wedding DJ`
- `Squamish, BC`
- An unmistakable wedding context
- A human, outcome-led headline
- One dominant action: `Check your date`
- A clear secondary action: `Book a Sound Check` or the current approved consultation language
- Real photographic evidence

No visitor should need to understand the deck metaphor before they can use the site.

### Primary journey

```text
Recognize the service
        ↓
Feel the difference
        ↓
See real proof
        ↓
Understand Patrick's role
        ↓
Check the date
        ↓
Book the conversation
```

### Existing capabilities to preserve

- Public availability checking and all governed availability states
- Consultation booking flow
- Contact and inquiry paths
- Google rating and review-count freshness
- Analytics and conversion tracking
- SEO routes, structured data, metadata, and local authority content
- Venue and story content
- Accessibility, reduced-motion behavior, and keyboard support
- Mobile usability and page speed

The redesign changes the presentation system. It does not casually rewrite proven business infrastructure.

## 4. The anti-template test

If a component could comfortably appear on a SaaS startup website, redesign it.

### Retire as default language

- Large rounded cards repeated throughout a page
- Floating translucent panels
- Glassmorphism
- Soft radial glows used as atmosphere
- Generic gold-on-black luxury styling
- Pill buttons used for every action
- Perfectly symmetrical three-column sections
- Small uppercase eyebrow labels above every heading
- Generic line icons
- Feature-card grids used to explain human services
- Gradient text
- Excessive whitespace with little visual tension
- The repeated sequence `hero → cards → testimonial grid → FAQ → gradient CTA`
- Identical section entrances and easing curves
- Every photograph contained inside a pristine rounded rectangle
- Copy composed mainly of polished three-part slogans

### Do not replace those patterns with clichés

- No neon equalizer wallpaper
- No fake nightclub lasers
- No turntable icon in every section
- No random records added merely because Patrick is a DJ
- No graffiti language without a real connection to the brand
- No festival-rave identity that obscures weddings
- No generic black-and-red “EDM” look
- No fake analog distress applied uniformly to everything
- No autoplay music
- No interaction that requires musical knowledge to navigate

### Designed disorder rule

Asymmetry, overlap, roughness, oversized type, and industrial elements must be composed deliberately. Every expressive choice must preserve hierarchy, readability, and a clear next action.

## 5. Visual system

### 5.1 Colour

The colour system should feel printed, physical, and local—not luminous and synthetic.

#### Core roles

| Role | Working character | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night | Soft near-black | Main nighttime fields, navigation, equipment surfaces |
| Paper | Warm off-white | Editorial sections, captions, readable long-form content |
| Signal yellow | Existing Howe Sound energy | Primary actions, cue states, active markers |
| Ultramarine | Cold Sea-to-Sky counterpoint | Section fields, duotones, links, editorial interruptions |
| Signal red | Rare peak-energy accent | Live state, warnings, playheads, small moments of emphasis |
| Metal grey | Equipment neutral | Rules, labels, inactive controls, technical diagrams |

Final accessible values must be chosen during implementation. Colour may not be used as the sole carrier of state.

#### Colour behavior

- Prefer flat ink-like fields to ambient gradients.
- Allow abrupt colour changes between chapters of the night.
- Use signal yellow for a decision or active state, not as decoration everywhere.
- Reserve signal red so it retains urgency and energy.
- Use monochrome and duotone photography selectively.
- Preserve enough neutral space for wedding imagery and emotional copy to breathe.

### 5.2 Typography

Typography must create tension between music culture and professional clarity.

#### Type roles

1. **Display voice:** Large, condensed or editorial, unapologetic, capable of breaking the grid.
2. **Human reading voice:** Warm, highly legible, and comfortable for planning information and stories.
3. **Equipment voice:** Condensed or monospaced labeling for channels, timecodes, locations, status, and metadata.

#### Rules

- Replace Geist as the dominant visible brand voice.
- Use no more than three type families and preferably two families with a technical style/weight.
- Display words may crop at the viewport or cross image boundaries when comprehension remains intact.
- Technical labeling must communicate real metadata or hierarchy; it cannot become decorative noise.
- Sentence case should remain the default for long copy.
- All-caps labels must remain short.
- Typography must be tested at 320px width and 200% zoom before approval.

No final font is selected by this constitution. Licensing, loading cost, language support, and performance must be evaluated during the homepage prototype.

### 5.3 Shape and structure

- Default surfaces use square or minimally softened corners.
- Large rounded containers become rare and intentional.
- Use rules, rails, notches, screws, channel divisions, tape, labels, and crop marks as structural motifs.
- Borders should resemble equipment separation or print registration, not generic card outlines.
- Depth comes primarily from layering, photography, type, and material—not box shadows.
- One strong composition is preferable to six small cards.

### 5.4 Texture

Texture should suggest an object that has been used, not a filter applied by software.

Preferred sources:

- Scanned paper and tape
- Equipment wear
- Photographic grain
- Halftone separations
- Photocopier or risograph artifacts
- Flight-case surfaces and labeling
- Handwritten notes
- Real dust, scratches, fingerprints, and imperfect ink

Texture must remain subtle around forms, dates, body copy, and accessibility-critical controls.

## 6. Interface grammar: DJ hardware with meaning

Hardware references are strongest when they explain something.

### Vocabulary

`CUE · PLAY · LOAD · GAIN · FILTER · MASTER · CH 01 · CH 02 · SIGNAL · LIVE · TRACK · OUTPUT`

Use this vocabulary as supporting language. Do not rename every conventional action until it becomes unclear.

### Component mapping

| Website need | HSDJ expression | Required meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary availability action | Backlit cue/performance button | Check a wedding date |
| Availability result | Signal/status module | Available, unavailable, or manual follow-up stated plainly |
| Page/chapter progress | Playhead on a waveform or track rail | Current position in the night/story |
| Wedding flow explanation | Channel faders or level rails | Relative emphasis and progression, not fake controls |
| Story/review browsing | Previous/next track logic | Move through real evidence |
| Selected state | Illuminated pad/LED plus text | Current route, filter, package, or story |
| Photo sequence | Contact sheet, sleeve, or track list | Editorial narrative |
| Form submit | Tactile primary control | Plain-language submission remains visible |
| Section divider | Waveform/time ruler/channel rail | Rhythm and transition |
| Trust metrics | Equipment specification plate | Rating, review count, years, and location |

### Semantic faders

Faders and meters may be used only when their values are defensible and immediately understandable.

Good uses:

- Showing the changing energy of ceremony, cocktails, dinner, and dance floor
- Showing how much guidance a couple wants to provide
- Demonstrating that club energy is an optional late-night destination
- Comparing planning support, musical freedom, or MC involvement when those values are explained

Bad uses:

- Invented percentages
- Claims that imply a measurable guarantee
- Decorative sliders that do nothing
- Controls that appear editable but are not
- Humor that insults a couple's musical taste

### Plain-language pairing

Every metaphorical control must have a literal label.

Examples:

- `CHECK DATE` may be styled as a cue button.
- `SIGNAL: AVAILABLE` must also state that the selected date currently appears available.
- `BOOK A SOUND CHECK` must explain that it is a relaxed planning conversation.

## 7. Photography and artwork

### Photography principle

**You were not supposed to see the photographer. You were supposed to be at the party.**

### Required image mix

- Packed floors and visible emotional release
- Patrick working, listening, preparing, and interacting
- Ceremony and quiet planning moments
- Hands, faces, cables, speakers, decks, and room details
- Venue exteriors and unmistakable Sea-to-Sky geography
- Load-in, sound check, and behind-the-scenes evidence
- Motion blur, direct flash, sweat, imperfect crops, and photographs with obstruction
- A smaller number of polished hero-grade wedding images for contrast

### Treatment rules

- Let important photographs run full-bleed or collide with typography.
- Use sequences to show a night changing, rather than treating every image as a standalone portrait.
- Preserve faces and moments over rigid aspect-ratio consistency.
- Use captions with real venues, dates, observations, or musical context where permission allows.
- Duotone and halftone treatments are supporting devices; original documentary colour must remain present.
- Never generate fake couples, fake events, fake crowds, or fake venues.

### Physical artifact library

Build an HSDJ-owned source library from:

- Patrick's handwritten set lists and preparation notes
- Old Howe Sound DJ flyers and Wix-era material worth preserving
- Real equipment labels, stickers, cases, and cables
- Crate or playlist screenshots that can be shown legally and usefully
- Venue maps, schedules, and load-in markings with private information removed
- Records, sleeves, and music objects Patrick actually owns
- Scans of tape, paper, stamps, marks, and annotations made for the project

Real artifacts outrank generated approximations. The imperfections must belong to the business.

### Artwork rule

Artwork must either:

1. Reveal something true about Patrick, the couple, the music, or the location; or
2. Improve navigation, hierarchy, or the narrative of the night.

If it does neither, it is decoration and should be removed.

## 8. Motion and sound

### Motion vocabulary

Motion should feel musical and mechanical:

- Buttons depress
- LEDs illuminate
- Playheads travel
- Faders settle
- Numbers count with purpose
- Images cut, punch, or crossfade
- Text enters in deliberate beats
- Meters respond to a meaningful input

Avoid universal float-and-fade animation.

### Timing

- Use two or three motion rhythms rather than one generic easing curve.
- Quick mechanical actions should feel immediate.
- Chapter transitions may be more cinematic but must not block scrolling.
- Avoid scroll-jacking.
- The user controls pace.

### Responsive meters

VU meters may respond subtly to scrolling, pointer movement, or opted-in media. They must not create false audio activity that confuses users, and they must have a static reduced-motion form.

### Sound

- Never autoplay sound.
- Audio must be explicitly initiated and visibly stoppable.
- Volume state must persist while navigating where technically reasonable.
- Controls require text alternatives and keyboard operation.
- A silent visitor must receive the complete experience.

## 9. Copy constitution

The website should sound like Patrick—not an agency, a wedding template, or a language model polishing every sentence into the same rhythm.

### Voice

- First person where Patrick is genuinely speaking
- Direct, warm, observant, and confident
- Specific about what happens before and during a wedding
- Musically fluent without gatekeeping
- Comfortable expressing an opinion
- Reassuring without becoming soft or generic
- Energetic without promising a nightclub to every couple

### Prefer

- Real moments
- Concrete actions
- Short sentences with varied rhythm
- Language already used by couples
- Venue and regional specificity
- Honest distinctions between parts of the night
- Clear statements of what Patrick does and does not do

### Avoid

- “Elevated,” “curated,” “bespoke,” and “unforgettable” without evidence
- “Your X. Your Y. Your Z.” constructions
- Repeated three-part slogans
- “Seamless experience” as unsupported filler
- Formulaic reassurance slogans repeated across routes
- Generic superlatives
- False scarcity
- Pressure tactics
- Pretending every wedding wants the same intensity
- Suggesting drum & bass, house, disco, and throwbacks are all mixed together indiscriminately

### Music-positioning rule

Musical breadth must be framed as responsiveness to the couple and the room.

Club genres should be presented as an available direction—often for a later peak—not as the default identity of the entire reception.

### Headline standard

A homepage headline must:

- Express an emotionally recognizable truth
- Connect the couple, their people, and the room
- Sound natural when spoken aloud by Patrick
- Avoid wedding-industry clichés
- Avoid describing the interface concept
- Work without its supporting paragraph

The interface can feel like a deck. The headline still needs to sell the human outcome.

## 10. Homepage architecture: the night as a set

The homepage should progress like the energy curve of a wedding rather than a collection of marketing modules.

### CH 00 — Arrival

**Job:** Immediate recognition and emotional hook.

- One dominant real image or tightly directed photo sequence
- Howe Sound DJ, Squamish, and wedding context
- Final approved human headline
- `Check your date` as the dominant action
- Consultation as secondary
- Minimal equipment language

### CH 01 — Sound check

**Job:** Establish capable, prepared competence before spectacle.

- What Patrick handles
- Ceremony-to-reception scope
- Planning and communication proof
- A human glimpse of Patrick preparing or working
- Avoid a feature-card grid

### CH 02 — The build

**Job:** Demonstrate that the night has shape.

- Ceremony, cocktails, dinner, speeches, first dance, open floor
- A semantic timeline, channel strip, or energy rail
- Explicitly show that different moments require different musical judgment
- Connect technical control to emotional ease

### CH 03 — Read the room

**Job:** Explain Patrick's musical intelligence.

- The couple's taste is the source material
- Requests and guest response matter
- Patrick mixes live and makes decisions in the room
- Late-night club energy is an available destination, not an all-night assumption
- Use one meaningful interactive demonstration only if it improves understanding

### CH 04 — Peak

**Job:** Make the outcome felt rather than claimed.

- Large, imperfect dance-floor photography
- Faster visual rhythm
- Real customer language
- Motion or waveform treatment
- Rating and current review count as hard proof
- No wall of tiny testimonial cards

### CH 05 — The operator

**Job:** Let couples meet Patrick.

- First-person perspective
- Real opinions and working principles
- Local roots
- Quiet portrait or behind-the-scenes contrast after the peak
- Link to the full story

### CH 06 — The room remembers

**Job:** Deepen trust.

- Two or three substantial reviews with names and context
- Selected stories or venues
- Evidence of planning care and dance-floor results
- Editorial layout rather than a card carousel by default

### CH 07 — Encore

**Job:** Convert without returning to template language.

- Clear availability action
- Sound Check/consultation explanation
- No-pressure reassurance
- A visually distinctive final composition inspired by an equipment plate, gig bill, or track ending
- Footer remains usable and conventional enough to navigate

## 11. Sitewide architecture

Not every page should repeat the full homepage performance.

### High-expression pages

- Homepage
- Weddings
- About
- Reviews
- Selected wedding stories

These pages may use larger typography, richer motion, hardware-derived composition, and documentary sequences.

### Medium-expression pages

- Squamish wedding DJ
- Venue hub and venue profiles
- Packages/services
- Contact

These pages retain the brand language while prioritizing comparison, confidence, and completion.

### Reading-first pages

- Guides
- FAQs
- Planning resources
- Legal or operational content

These pages use the typography, colour, labeling, and small structural motifs without compromising reading comfort.

### Repetition rule

Shared brand grammar does not mean identical page composition. Reuse tokens and behavior; do not stamp the same hero, three-card grid, and CTA well onto every route.

## 12. Accessibility, performance, and trust constraints

The redesign fails if its personality makes the site harder to use.

### Accessibility

- Meet WCAG AA contrast for text and controls.
- Preserve visible focus states.
- All primary actions must be keyboard reachable.
- Do not encode status with colour alone.
- Provide reduced-motion equivalents for every branded motion pattern.
- Decorative waveforms and meters must be hidden appropriately from assistive technology.
- Interactive metaphors require familiar labels and clear states.
- Body copy remains readable at narrow widths and 200% zoom.
- Touch targets must remain comfortably usable.

### Performance

- No autoplay video or audio.
- Avoid heavy 3D libraries and continuous canvas animation.
- Use CSS and small purposeful scripts for most mechanical motion.
- Load rich interaction only where it materially improves the page.
- Preserve responsive image optimization.
- Font choices must not create a slow or unstable first render.
- The live Google review count must remain lightweight and degrade gracefully to a current governed fallback.

### Trust

- Maintain clear service, location, contact, and booking information.
- Use real reviews with attribution.
- Keep availability language accurate and governed.
- Do not imply guarantees the business cannot make.
- Do not hide practical information inside novelty interactions.
- Never make a couple feel tested on musical knowledge.

## 13. Component hierarchy

The visual idea requires restraint. Components are divided into three levels.

### Level 1 — Signature components

Use prominently and repeatedly enough to become recognizable:

- Cue-style primary action
- Signal/status availability language
- Track rail or waveform chapter divider
- Equipment-spec trust plate
- Documentary photo sequence
- Technical metadata labels

### Level 2 — Supporting components

Use selectively:

- Semantic fader or energy curve
- Contact-sheet gallery
- Backlit selected states
- Track-style story/review navigation
- Duotone image interruption
- Equipment-panel form treatment

### Level 3 — Experiments

Prototype before adoption:

- Vinyl sleeve interaction
- Reactive VU meters
- Scroll-linked playhead
- Rhythm-timed page transitions
- Persistent opt-in audio player

The homepage prototype should use at most one Level 3 experiment. A proprietary visual language comes from consistency, not an accumulation of tricks.

The prohibition on experimentation does not make conventional composition the safe fallback. Level 1 components must themselves be composed in non-template ways. Restraint means fewer ideas, pushed further—not conventional layouts with fewer effects.

## 14. Prototype and rollout plan

### Phase 0 — Preserve the baseline

- Record current conversion paths, analytics events, metadata, and page-speed baseline.
- Capture desktop and mobile references of all key states.
- Inventory reusable photography and real artifacts.
- Identify the content and functionality that must not regress.

### Phase 1A — CH 00 composition accreditation

- Follow the Anti-Template Design Execution Protocol's pre-code stages.
- Produce the approved composition artifacts for `CH 00 — Arrival` only.
- Explore genuinely different structural species at approximately 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844.
- Use real HSDJ photography and the best available source artifacts.
- Establish the intended desktop/mobile relationship.
- Obtain Patrick's explicit creative approval before production implementation.

### Phase 1B — CH 01 accreditation

- Begin `CH 01 — Sound Check` only after CH 00 has passed its composition, audit, conversion, and human approval gates.
- Do not treat CH 00 approval as authorization for any later chapter.

### Phase 1C — Subsequent chapters

- Accredit one chapter at a time through the same protocol.
- Introduce no more than one Level 3 experiment across the initial homepage system unless Patrick explicitly authorizes another.

### Phase 1D — Assembled homepage validation

- Assemble chapters only after their individual approval.
- Confirm that the full page behaves as one energy arc rather than a sequence of independent designs.
- Validate the real availability path, type system, accessible colour values, desktop/mobile relationship, and chapter transitions together.
- Do not begin sitewide migration until the assembled homepage passes Phase 2.

### Phase 2 — Validate the system

Evaluate the prototype against:

- Five-second comprehension
- Patrick recognizability
- Squamish recognizability
- Wedding-service clarity
- Date-check visibility
- Emotional response
- Mobile usability
- Reduced motion
- Performance and layout stability

Do not expand sitewide until the homepage passes.

### Phase 3 — Build reusable primitives

- Tokens for colour, type, borders, rhythm, and motion
- Cue buttons and control states
- Chapter headers and track rails
- Equipment plates and proof treatments
- Editorial photo compositions
- Reading-first content patterns
- Forms and availability states

### Phase 4 — Route migration

Recommended order:

1. Weddings
2. About
3. Reviews
4. Contact and availability surfaces
5. Squamish and venue pages
6. Packages/services
7. Stories
8. Guides and FAQ

### Phase 5 — Copy pass

- Remove repeated AI-like cadence sitewide.
- Consolidate overlapping claims.
- Introduce Patrick's first-person voice.
- Preserve approved customer language and factual claims.
- Re-test headings in context rather than in isolation.

### Phase 6 — Final validation

- Functional tests
- Accessibility audit
- Mobile and desktop visual review
- Reduced-motion review
- Performance comparison with baseline
- SEO and structured-data checks
- Analytics event verification
- Public availability end-to-end verification

## 15. Acceptance criteria

The redesign is successful when:

- A visitor identifies the service, location, and next step immediately.
- The site could not plausibly belong to a DJ in another city with the name changed.
- Patrick feels present before the About section.
- Real weddings carry more visual authority than interface decoration.
- DJ hardware informs behavior rather than acting as a costume.
- The visual system contains both beautiful and industrial elements.
- The night visibly progresses from anticipation to peak energy.
- Musical range feels reassuring rather than intimidating.
- The availability and consultation journeys remain simple.
- Mobile feels intentionally composed, not like compressed desktop.
- Reduced-motion mode remains complete and attractive.
- No major section defaults to a generic rounded-card grid.
- Copy sounds natural when read aloud.
- Performance, accessibility, SEO, and analytics do not regress materially.

### The final gut check

Ask these questions before approving any page:

1. Could this appear unchanged on a SaaS site?
2. Could this belong to any wedding DJ?
3. Is the DJ reference meaningful or decorative?
4. Is Squamish present as lived context rather than scenery?
5. Does Patrick remain recognizably himself while fully inhabiting the visual energy?
6. Can a couple find the next step without decoding the design?
7. Is this using a real artifact where a fake one would have been easier?

If the answer exposes genericity, gimmick, or friction, revise the page.

The first prototype can begin with existing photography. To make the final identity genuinely proprietary, collect the following without cleaning or redesigning it first:

- 10–20 old flyers, screenshots, business cards, or Wix-era pieces with surviving character
- Handwritten set lists or music-planning notes
- Close photographs of decks, mixer markings, buttons, LEDs, cases, tape, stickers, and cables
- Setup and sound-check photographs
- Imperfect dance-floor photographs or phone images
- Venue exteriors, weather, mountains, roads, and load-in moments
- Records, sleeves, or music objects that genuinely matter to Patrick
- Words and phrases Patrick naturally uses with couples
- A short list of design/music references Patrick loves and strongly dislikes

Private names, phone numbers, schedules, and client details must be removed before public use.

## 17. First design decision

Do not begin with a sitewide CSS restyle.

Begin with Phase 0 source reconnaissance. Then complete the Anti-Template Design Execution Protocol's pre-code mission for `CH 00 — Arrival` only.

CH 00 must prove:

1. A human, outcome-led opening composition
2. A cue-style availability action that remains immediately understandable
3. Real HSDJ documentary photography
4. A recognizable relationship between desktop and mobile
5. Enough authorship to justify continuing the visual language

Do not design the energy curve, Peak, Encore, or another homepage chapter during this mission. Those elements receive their own later composition, audit, conversion, and Patrick-approval gates.

The Anti-Template Design Execution Protocol governs sequencing wherever an older statement in this constitution could be interpreted more broadly.
