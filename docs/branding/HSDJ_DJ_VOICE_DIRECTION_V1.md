# Howe Sound DJ — DJ Voice Direction V1

**Status:** Research direction / Patrick review required  
**Production copy:** Not approved  
**Purpose:** Restore Patrick's real wedding-DJ voice before the next copy pass.

## The voice

Patrick should sound like someone who has loaded in, solved a microphone problem, listened properly to a couple, watched a room change, and known when to leave a great track alone.

The tone is conversational, specific, playful, music-literate, direct, and imperfectly human. It can have an opinion. It does not perform “premium.” It earns confidence through useful detail and lived judgment.

## What the voice talks about

- What the couple actually wants the room to feel like.
- The difference between ceremony, cocktails, dinner, speeches, and the open floor.
- The small decisions that keep a wedding moving without calling attention to themselves.
- Music the couple loves, music their people respond to, and the live judgment that connects the two.
- Club energy as an available late-night destination when the brief calls for it.
- Squamish rooms, weather, access, sound limits, timing, and other local realities Patrick genuinely knows.
- The relief of having one prepared person listening to the whole event.

## Sentence behaviour

- Use contractions.
- Let short and long sentences sit beside each other.
- Prefer a concrete observation to an abstract promise.
- Say what Patrick does in plain language.
- Leave a little texture in the phrasing; do not polish every line into a slogan.
- Use first person when Patrick is actually speaking.
- Let customer quotes remain customer language, with enough context to mean something.

## Do not publish this style

Reject these lines and their close relatives:

- `ONE NIGHT / FIVE DIFFERENT ROOMS`
- `YOU DON'T DROP THE PEAK AT DINNER.`
- `THE QUIET WORK BEHIND THE NIGHT.`
- `WHEN EVERYBODY GETS IT.`

Also reject:

- manifesto copy;
- balanced three-part slogans;
- `X isn't Y, it's Z` constructions;
- agency language and premium-service clichés;
- abstractions that could belong to any wedding supplier;
- tough-sounding club copy that makes Patrick seem inflexible;
- genre lists that imply drum & bass, house, disco, and throwbacks are mixed together all night;
- orphaned review fragments such as `RUN, DON'T WALK` used as a major headline without the couple, wedding, or reason behind it.

## Review quotation discipline

A review excerpt needs enough surrounding information to preserve the customer's meaning. Keep the name, wedding or venue context where permission allows, and the specific reason for the praise. A loud fragment is not stronger evidence when it could describe anyone.

## Provisional experiments — not approved copy

These lines exist only to test the direction in later compositions. They are not authorized for production.

### Opening-direction experiments

- `A wedding that sounds like you—and feels like everybody came ready.`
- `Bring your people. Bring your taste. I'll take care of how the night moves.`
- `You know the songs. I know what to do with the room.`

### Musical-range experiments

- `Want the last hour to feel like a proper club night? House, drum & bass, or wherever your crowd wants to go—we can build to it.`
- `Dinner doesn't need the same music as midnight. That's the point.`
- `Your favourites start the conversation. The room tells me where to take it.`

### Planning-direction experiments

- `I’ll sort the sound, the timing, and the handoffs before they become things you have to think about.`
- `Tell me what matters, what you never want to hear, and who is most likely to start the dance floor.`
- `Good planning should make the wedding feel less planned.`

## Read-aloud test

Before any line advances, Patrick should be able to say it naturally in a consultation without sounding like he is reading a campaign. If the line becomes smoother but less recognizably his, keep the rougher version.
