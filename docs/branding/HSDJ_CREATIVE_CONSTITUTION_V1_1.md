## 2. What the experiments established

The redesign has found its ingredients but not its finished art direction.

The working system is no longer adequately described as DJ hardware alone. It is the collision of:

- professional DJ machinery;
- gig-poster and fly-posting culture;
- real wedding documentary photography; and
- Squamish and Sea-to-Sky specificity.

CH00 V3 proved that machine, poster, and human material can coexist while retaining working VU, fader, navigation, and availability-control logic. It also exposed the remaining risk: poster treatment can be layered over inherited website zoning without truly recomposing the viewport.

The next task is therefore not another isolated CH00 polish pass. It is to prove whether this language can sustain the whole homepage across different kinds of information and different emotional volumes.

## 3. Expanded creative thesis

### Primary thesis

**THE WEBSITE IS THE DECK.**

The interface derives from the behavior, construction, hierarchy, materials, and labeling of professional DJ equipment. Hardware must carry meaning rather than decorate a conventional website.

### Transforming thesis

**THE DECK HAS BEEN FLY-POSTED.**

The machine is no longer pristine. Poster planes, torn documentary photographs, printed colour, real labels, venue evidence, and human marks have accumulated on it. The site should feel like a working instrument that has passed through actual nights, not a luxury interface themed after DJ equipment.

### Complete visual equation

```text
DJ machinery
    × gig-poster culture
    × real wedding documentary
    × Squamish / Sea-to-Sky specificity
= Howe Sound DJ
```

These are not four decorative themes. Together they form one authored system:

- **Machine** supplies behavior, control, rhythm, construction, and operational credibility.
- **Poster** supplies typographic force, colour, collision, imperfection, and cultural energy.
- **Documentary** supplies human truth, weddings, Patrick, emotion, and proof.
- **Squamish** makes the system local and proprietary through real places, weather, terrain, venues, travel, materials, and language.

If one layer disappears for too long, the system becomes generic: nightclub hardware, graphic-design fashion, wedding-template sentiment, or interchangeable tourism branding.

## 4. The two-gate model

The previous sequential rule combined two different decisions. V1.1 separates them.

### Gate A — Visual-system exploration

The project is authorized to compose `CH00` through `CH07` together as one full-homepage art-direction prototype.

The purpose is to answer:

- Can the language sustain an entire homepage?
- Can it change intensity without losing identity?
- Can planning, music, proof, Patrick, reviews, and conversion each have a distinct form?
- Does the page build, peak, release, and return like a night?
- Does the design escape the pattern of an expressive hero followed by generic sections?

Gate A permits provisional copy, provisional crops, non-production HTML/CSS/JS, and research-only compositions inside the designated prototype directory. It does not permit production changes.

### Gate B — Production accreditation

Production approval remains chapter-by-chapter and state-by-state.

Each chapter must still receive its own:

- composition judgment;
- Anti-Design Auditor review;
- Conversion Guardian review;
- desktop/mobile relationship approval;
- accessibility and reduced-motion definition;
- source and rights clearance;
- performance assessment;
- Patrick creative approval; and
- implementation certification.

Approval of the full-page art-direction prototype means only that the system is worth developing. It does not approve every chapter, copy line, image, behavior, or production implementation.

### Non-transfer rule

No approval transfers silently:

- CH00 approval does not approve CH01.
- Full-page visual-system approval does not accredit any chapter for production.
- Desktop approval does not approve mobile.
- A composition approval does not approve copy.
- A research asset does not become rights-cleared by appearing in a prototype.

## 5. Composition law

The full homepage must not become eight versions of CH00.

Shared grammar means recurring materials, behaviors, and attitudes—not repeated layout skeletons. Every chapter must change the relationship between machine, poster, documentary, and place according to its job.

### Required variation

Across the page, vary:

- density;
- scale;
- reading direction;
- proportion of machine to paper;
- proportion of photograph to typography;
- number of simultaneous planes;
- motion rhythm;
- amount of literal control behavior;
- degree of polish, damage, and quiet; and
- relationship between evidence and claim.

### Inherited-layout warning

Adding torn paper, colour, hardware, or grain over a recognizable hero, card grid, split section, testimonial carousel, or CTA well does not constitute recomposition.

When a familiar layout remains visible beneath the art direction, the chapter must be rebuilt at the structural level.

## 6. Colour doctrine V1.1

Yellow, cobalt, and red are physical graphic materials as well as interface colours.

They may behave like ink, paint, pasted paper, tape, misregistration, labels, overprint, or abrupt fields. They do not need to justify every appearance as a UI state.

### Two simultaneous colour systems

#### Machine signals

Inside controls, meters, status modules, and availability behavior:

- colour retains semantic discipline;
- active, warning, unavailable, focus, and success states remain understandable;
- colour is never the sole carrier of meaning; and
- the governed availability system may not be made ambiguous for visual effect.

#### Poster materials

Inside expressive composition:

- yellow, cobalt, red, black, and paper may occupy large areas;
- abrupt collisions are preferred to ambient gradients;
- colour may lead a chapter rather than merely accent it;
- overprint and misregistration may create tension; and
- neutral space is earned by the energy curve, not imposed as the default mark of professionalism.

### Colour failure modes

- Retreating to mostly black because it feels safely premium
- Using yellow only for buttons
- Reserving red so heavily that the poster system loses force
- Turning every chapter into the same three-colour balance
- Using low contrast as evidence of sophistication
- Allowing expressive colour to corrupt functional state meaning

## 7. Signature component hierarchy V1.1

The following are now Level 1 signature components. They are the established core grammar, not optional experiments.

### Level 1 — Signature system

- **Physical performance-pad navigation** — route names may live on the pad faces; the semantic link and accessible name remain explicit.
- **Physical CUE availability control** — the dominant date-check action must feel tactile while remaining unmistakably plain language.
- **Living stereo VU system** — a meaningful, restrained sign of live energy with a complete static reduced-motion state.
- **Fader/rail architecture** — controls, seams, timelines, chapter transitions, and information relationships may share one physical rail logic.
- **Machine substrate** — a continuous operational surface, not a collection of fake mixer cards.
- **Poster-plane typography** — type may crop, tear, misregister, cross planes, and become material while preserving reading order.
- **Documentary photographic collision** — real wedding and Patrick imagery may interrupt controls, type, and substrate rather than live in polite containers.

### Level 2 — Supporting system

- Gaffer-tape labels and handwritten annotations
- Real knobs, switches, jacks, cables, scales, and channel details with declared ancestry
- Contact sheets, sleeves, pasted evidence, and photocopy fragments
- Equipment specification plates for factual trust information
- Real waveform or track-rail treatments with defensible meaning
- Duotone, halftone, overprint, and photocopy transformations of approved sources
- Quiet paper/editorial fields used as deliberate release

### Level 3 — Controlled experiments

- Opt-in sound
- Scroll-linked playheads beyond the established rail behavior
- More complex reactive or pointer-driven systems
- Vinyl or sleeve mechanics that affect navigation
- Rhythm-timed chapter transitions

Level 3 remains limited. A Level 1 component may still be rejected when it is decorative, inauthentic, inaccessible, or merely placed beside a conventional web component.

### Sitewide action-control law

Every visible, branded primary or secondary action must be expressed through an approved piece of DJ or audio gear: CUE, PLAY, performance pad, transport key, illuminated switch, fader, or another control with declared ancestry and a function that matches the action. A generic pill, rounded rectangle, outline button, or card-shaped CTA is not an HSDJ action control.

- Date and availability actions use the physical CUE control unless Patrick explicitly approves another hardware mapping.
- Consultation or start actions use PLAY or another semantically defensible transport control.
- The physical control must carry the interaction. Placing a gear image beside or inside an otherwise conventional web button does not satisfy this rule.
- The plain-language action label, accessible name, focus state, keyboard behaviour, and minimum practical tap area remain mandatory.
- Native browser controls, invisible utility controls, and small close/disclosure controls may use conventional affordances when hardware styling would reduce clarity or accessibility.
- Any exception for a branded call to action requires Patrick's explicit, recorded approval.

## 8. Optical and physical-control discipline

Physical fidelity is judged by the perceived control surface, not the transparent asset bounds or mathematical box.

- Labels must be optically aligned to the visible face of a pad, CUE button, or PLAY control.
- Small manual offsets are acceptable and often necessary.
- Text placed on a physical control remains real semantic text where feasible.
- The control's function must be legible without recognizing the hardware reference.
- A photographed control beside a conventional button is not integration.
- Controls that look operable must either work or be clearly non-interactive and hidden from the accessibility tree.

Graphic judgment outranks coordinate purity.

## 9. Collage intensity system

Collage is a variable, not a sitewide filter.

### LOW intensity

Primarily machine and editorial composition.

Typical ingredients:

- one torn label;
- one hardware intrusion;
- one colour field;
- restrained photographic evidence; and
- breathing room that feels intentional rather than luxurious.

Use for planning-heavy, explanatory, intimate, or operational material.

### MEDIUM intensity

Machine and poster collide visibly.

Typical ingredients:

- several overlapping planes;
- hardware performing information work;
- documentary photography interrupting the system;
- stronger type-scale changes; and
- more active transitions.

Use for Build, Read the Room, selected proof, and transitions into or out of the peak.

### PEAK intensity

Full visual attack, earned by emotional content.

Typical ingredients:

- torn crowd photography;
- huge type;
- active VU behavior;
- high-force colour;
- hardware, waveform, print damage, and evidence colliding; and
- compressed visual rhythm with a still-clear reading path.

PEAK is not the default. It exists so the page has somewhere to go. `CH04 — Peak` should be allowed to exceed CH00.

### Intensity test

Intensity is not the number of effects. It is the amount of simultaneous visual force. If additional material does not increase emotional meaning, evidence, hierarchy, or musical rhythm, remove it.

## 11. Chapter-specific visual jobs

### CH00 — Arrival / 70

- Establish machine, poster, human, wedding, Patrick, and Squamish quickly.
- Keep the date-check action dominant.
- Create impact without spending the page's maximum volume.
- Avoid residual text-left/image-right or expressive-left/empty-right zoning.
- Treat the current headline as provisional.

### CH01 — Sound Check / 40

- Strip the system back.
- Let a real DJM channel strip or equivalent physical ancestor organize preparation and scope.
- Use a small number of real knobs or markers with white gaffer-tape labeling.
- Make competence tangible without returning to feature cards.

### CH02 — Build / 60

- Use faders, rails, or channels to shape the progression from ceremony through open floor.
- Treat time and changing responsibility as the composition.
- Make technical control serve emotional ease.
- Do not turn the sequence into an evenly spaced process diagram.

### CH03 — Read the Room / 70

- Use EQ, gain, filter, waveform, or cue logic only where it explains live judgment.
- Let wedding photographs interrupt or overtake the controls.
- Increase motion and visual responsiveness.
- Frame club energy as a possible late-night destination, never the all-night default.

### CH04 — Peak / 100

- Create the wildest composition on the homepage.
- Use the strongest approved crowd photography, torn flyer material, large type, active meters, and real review language.
- Allow meters to move toward yellow and red without implying a technical guarantee.
- Make proof part of the impact rather than placing testimonials beneath it.
- Maintain a recoverable reading order inside the attack.

### CH05 — Operator / 30

- Drop the volume sharply.
- Bring Patrick forward through a real portrait, working detail, first-person language, handwriting, equipment, paper, or one oversized physical element.
- Feel intimate and specific, not like an About-card interruption.
- Let imperfection and quiet do more work than effects.

### CH06 — Room Remembers / 50

- Treat reviews and stories as evidence left behind by the night.
- Explore poster fragments, taped proof, sleeves, contact sheets, annotations, or documentary sequences.
- Preserve attribution, legibility, truth, and context.
- Do not use testimonial cards or a conventional quote carousel.

### CH07 — Encore / 80

- Return decisively to the machine.
- Give the physical CUE availability control maximum clarity and presence.
- Resolve the page as a final poster or track ending.
- Let the footer inherit equipment specification-plate logic while remaining conventionally navigable.
- End with momentum, not a generic rounded CTA panel.

## 12. Explicit anti-AI copy mandate

Copy is part of the art direction and must survive the same training-set gravity audit as layout.

### Required behavior

- Sound like Patrick speaking to a couple, not a strategist describing a brand.
- Prefer concrete moments, real work, and specific observations.
- Vary sentence length and cadence.
- Let some lines be plain.
- Use customer language when it is true and attributable.
- Distinguish ceremony, dinner, speeches, first dance, open floor, and late-night peak rather than collapsing the night into one promise.
- Let the visual system carry some of the energy instead of forcing every line to perform.

### Rewrite triggers

Rewrite copy when it:

- sounds like a polished positioning statement;
- uses balanced three-part cadence by reflex;
- could sit unchanged on another wedding vendor's site;
- calls itself authentic, elevated, unforgettable, seamless, curated, bespoke, or premium instead of demonstrating the claim;
- converts Patrick into an agency voice;
- treats every wedding as a packed-club-floor brief;
- explains the visual metaphor instead of the service; or
- needs punctuation tricks to feel distinctive.

### Current CH00 headline status

`A packed dance floor that still feels like your wedding.`

**Status: PROVISIONAL / REWRITE REQUIRED**

It may remain in research prototypes as a spatial placeholder. It is not approved headline copy and must not be treated as settled while evaluating the visual system.

The full-page prototype is not authorized to solve final homepage copy. It may expose what the opening needs to say and record useful language for the later copy mission.

## 13. Source authenticity and rights

The source hierarchy from the Anti-Template Protocol remains binding.

Additional V1.1 requirements:

- Prefer Patrick's actual controller photographs and the approved source-library details over approximated knobs, pads, screens, or faders.
- Treat files labeled as screenshots according to their actual content, provenance, and permission—not as disposable references.
- Do not lose or replace approved source material merely because a newer collage exists.
- Every transformed photograph must remain traceable to its original.
- Prototypes must label unresolved licensing and permission questions.
- No research hardware photograph, font, texture, review, or wedding image enters production without clearance.
- Never fabricate documentary proof.

Squamish must appear through real specificity, not generic mountain mood.

## 14. Accessibility, performance, and conversion remain constitutional

The wider exploration authority does not weaken the original constraints.

- A visitor must understand wedding DJ, Howe Sound DJ, Squamish, and the next action within the opening experience.
- Availability and consultation must retain plain-language labels.
- Navigation must remain semantic and keyboard operable.
- Mobile must be recomposed, not stacked.
- Reduced motion must remain complete and intentional.
- Decorative machine and collage material must not pollute the accessibility tree.
- No autoplay audio or video.
- No heavy continuous animation used to prove creative ambition.
- Rating, review count, reviews, availability, and business claims must remain governed and accurate.
- Poster density must yield around forms and essential reading.

Visual force is not permission to make couples decode the service.

## 17. Final V1.1 gut check

Before presenting the full-page art-direction prototype, ask:

1. Does the homepage feel like one authored set with an energy curve?
2. Did we create eight distinct chapter behaviors rather than eight CH00 variants?
3. Is the machine meaningful and physically credible?
4. Has the deck genuinely been fly-posted, or merely decorated with poster graphics?
5. Are real weddings and Patrick doing more emotional work than interface effects?
6. Is Squamish present as lived evidence?
7. Does CH04 exceed CH00 without making the rest of the page feel dead?
8. Does CH05 create a real human release?
9. Can a couple still identify the service and act without decoding the system?
10. Is any section visibly descended from a generic web template?
11. Does any copy sound like a language model delivering brand strategy?
12. Has any research decision been mistaken for production approval?

If the answer reveals genericity, repetition, inauthenticity, or hidden conversion friction, revise before presentation.
