# Howe Sound DJ Tier S Website Content and Presentation Audit V1

**Status:** Strategic audit and production brief  
**Owner:** Howe Sound DJ  
**Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Scope:** Current production website content, page architecture, visual repetition, evidence, conversion, mobile implications, and alignment with the approved creative system  
**Production mutation:** None. This document records findings and recommended work; it does not approve implementation.

## Governing authorities

This audit uses two primary documents:

1. `Questions for a TIER S website.txt`
2. `HSDJ_CREATIVE_CONSTITUTION_V1_1.md`

The first document decides whether a section deserves to exist. The Creative Constitution decides whether the section is presented in a recognizably Howe Sound DJ way.

Neither document is optional. A section can be strategically useful and still fail the creative system. A section can also look striking while contributing nothing to trust, understanding, proof, or conversion.

The combined governing question is:

> Does this help the right couple understand, trust, remember, and want Howe Sound DJ—and is it expressed through an authored combination of machine, poster, documentary wedding evidence, and real Squamish specificity?

---

## Executive judgment

The website has found a strong creative language, but it has not yet applied that language with enough strategic variation across the full site.

The homepage is the clearest expression of the brand. It uses physical controls, documentary photography, poster typography, local language, real reviews, and a visible conversion path. Several lines sound direct and human. The physical navigation, CUE availability control, living VU system, fader architecture, documentary collisions, and Patrick section are genuine signature assets.

The primary sitewide problem is not a lack of design effort. It is **repetition of one interior-page structure**:

- dark equipment backdrop;
- large display headline;
- introductory paragraph;
- repeated card or grid sections;
- repeated bordered DJ-art panels; and
- a familiar CTA panel at the bottom.

Seventeen interior pages begin from essentially the same hero scaffold, and eighteen routes use the shared `hsdj-interior` treatment. This makes the brand visible, but it does not satisfy the Constitution’s variation requirement. The result risks becoming “the homepage theme applied to every page” rather than distinct page compositions built from the same creative grammar.

The second major issue is editorial repetition. The current source repeatedly relies on the vocabulary of **flow**, **pacing**, **seamless**, **arc**, and related strategic language. A source-wide scan found approximately:

- 151 uses of “flow”;
- 117 uses of “pacing”;
- 20 uses of “seamless”;
- 11 uses of “Atmosphere Arc”;
- 8 uses of “unforgettable”; and
- several uses of “curated” and “bespoke.”

Not every occurrence is visible marketing copy, but the frequency confirms a pattern. This is exactly the language-model gravity identified by both governing documents. Many sections explain the brand strategy instead of letting real work, moments, reviews, equipment knowledge, and photographs demonstrate it.

The third major issue is page length and mission drift. Several pages contain six to eight sections when their visitor has one dominant question. Contact, FAQ, location pillars, reviews, and some editorial hubs repeat propositions already established elsewhere. The site needs fewer sections with stronger jobs—not more decorated surfaces.

### Overall decision

**Keep the creative system. Recompose the interior pages. Tighten the language. Increase real proof. Reduce repeated sections.**

The correct next phase is not another sitewide styling pass. It is a controlled page-by-page accreditation process in which each page receives its own mission, evidence plan, intensity, architecture, and conversion path.

## What is already working

### 1. The service is identifiable

The opening experience communicates wedding DJ, Squamish, music, and availability. Navigation and conversion labels remain understandable even when presented through physical controls.

### 2. The signature control system is real

The performance-pad navigation, physical CUE control, living stereo VU meter, fader logic, and cassette/deck conversion components behave as a system rather than isolated decoration.

### 3. The homepage contains differentiated chapter behaviour

The homepage is not merely a card stack. The Build, Sound Check, Read the Room, Operator, reviews, video, and Encore areas have different compositions and emotional jobs.

### 4. Patrick is present as a person

The strongest personal statement is:

> “The DJ you plan with is the DJ who shows up.”

This is specific, credible, relevant to booking, and difficult for an agency-style DJ company to copy honestly.

### 5. The website includes useful operational detail

Ceremony microphones, speeches, venue logistics, musical range, must-plays, do-not-plays, MC support, sound zones, and local travel realities give the site more substance than generic wedding language.

### 6. Conversion actions are explicit

“Check your date” and “Book a Consult” are plain-language actions. The controls may be expressive, but their functions do not require knowledge of DJ hardware.

### 7. Recent interior typography corrections improved legibility

Interior headings are now smaller, less luminous, and less prone to breaking individual words. The homepage CTA/VU collision has also been addressed across desktop and mobile widths.

## Sitewide failures to correct

## 1. Shared grammar has become shared layout

### Finding

Most interior pages use the same opening scaffold and the same family of sections, panels, grids, and finale treatments. This violates the Constitution’s composition law:

> Shared grammar means recurring materials, behaviours, and attitudes—not repeated layout skeletons.

### Why it matters

Different pages serve different psychological jobs. A Reviews page should feel like evidence left behind by real nights. A Contact page should reduce friction. An About page should create human intimacy. A Packages page should make comparison easy. Giving all of them the same layout reduces clarity and makes the brand system feel templated.

### Required correction

Create distinct page archetypes:

- **Service pages:** operational deck, coverage, decisions, live mixing, conversion.
- **Proof pages:** documentary evidence, review fragments, contact sheets, named attribution.
- **Person page:** low-intensity portrait, handwriting, working details, first-person voice.
- **Decision pages:** restrained machine interface, comparison, questions, next actions.
- **Editorial pages:** print-led reading, documentary photographs, clear article hierarchy.
- **Local pages:** venue and place evidence, travel realities, named details, maps or contextual imagery where useful.

Do not use one universal interior hero or one universal card system.

## 2. Equipment is too often a backdrop instead of an information system

### Finding

Mixer artwork appears behind many panels regardless of what the section is explaining.

### Why it matters

The Constitution requires hardware to carry meaning. Repeated mixer imagery becomes wallpaper. Wallpaper makes the equipment less credible and eventually less visible.

### Required correction

Use equipment according to function:

- channel strips for coverage or service scope;
- faders and rails for progression or comparison;
- cue controls for choices and next steps;
- VU meters for real evidence of live energy;
- EQ or filter controls for live judgment and musical direction;
- specification plates for factual trust information;
- contact sheets and taped fragments for reviews and stories.

If no equipment metaphor helps the visitor understand the section, use documentary photography, paper, type, or deliberate quiet instead.

## 3. Copy frequently explains the strategy

### Finding

The site repeatedly uses language such as “flow,” “pacing,” “arc,” “energy,” “intentional,” and “seamless.” Some of it is accurate, but accumulated repetition makes the copy sound like a strategist describing the brand.

### Why it matters

Couples do not buy an “atmosphere arc.” They want their vows heard, their music respected, their guests engaged, their venue handled properly, and a DJ they trust.

### Required correction

Replace strategic abstractions with:

- a moment;
- a decision Patrick makes;
- a real planning detail;
- a specific result;
- attributable customer language; or
- a direct statement Patrick would naturally say.

Example:

- Weak: “The evening follows one cohesive atmosphere arc.”
- Stronger: “Dinner can stay relaxed. When your people are ready to dance, I already know where we are going next.”

## 4. Proof is present but too conventionally presented

### Finding

The dedicated Reviews page uses a conventional grid of testimonial cards. This directly conflicts with the CH06 instruction not to use testimonial cards or a conventional quote carousel.

### Why it matters

Real reviews are one of the strongest commercial assets on the site. Presenting them like standard website cards makes true evidence feel designed into generic marketing.

### Required correction

Recompose reviews as evidence left behind by the night:

- named quote fragments on taped proof;
- full quotes that open from those fragments;
- contact-sheet or sleeve structures;
- real wedding images tied to the emotional subject of a quote;
- handwriting or annotation used sparingly;
- review themes demonstrated through the actual words rather than summarized into marketing cards.

Attribution and complete readable text must remain accessible.

## 5. Several pages contain link sections with no persuasive job

### Finding

Some hubs and pillars include paragraphs whose main purpose is to send visitors to other internal pages. They read like internal-link infrastructure rather than useful content.

### Why it matters

The Tier S framework says a section must create desire, establish trust, explain the service, demonstrate expertise, remove an objection, provide proof, or make the next step easier. A paragraph containing several SEO-shaped links often does none of these convincingly.

### Required correction

An internal link must be the natural answer to a visitor’s next question. If a section only exists to distribute links, replace it with one purposeful decision module or remove it.

## 6. Mobile is responsive, but not always recomposed

### Finding

The current global system reduces type and stacks layouts at smaller widths. Recent checks show improved legibility, but the Constitution requires mobile to be recomposed rather than merely stacked.

### Required correction

For every accredited page, define:

- mobile reading order;
- which artwork survives;
- which equipment becomes a smaller functional control;
- which overlapping planes are removed;
- where conversion appears;
- maximum useful headline height; and
- the mobile-specific emotional beat.

Desktop approval must never transfer automatically to mobile.

## 7. Local specificity sometimes remains asserted rather than demonstrated

### Finding

The site frequently states that Patrick is local and understands corridor venues. The strongest instances mention travel, weather, load-in, spaces, microphones, and guest movement. Weaker instances repeat “local familiarity” without new proof.

### Required correction

Every local or venue page should contain at least one defensible, venue-relevant operational insight and one real source of local evidence. If that evidence is unavailable, say less rather than inventing specificity.

## 8. Conversion is clear but repeated too uniformly

### Finding

Many pages use the same CTADuo near the top and again in the same finale panel.

### Why it matters

Consistency is useful, but repeating the same conversion module can make every page feel mechanically assembled. It can also ask visitors to make the same decision before the page has earned it.

### Required correction

Choose one primary conversion action for each page:

- Packages: send date and likely scope.
- Reviews: check availability after proof.
- About: book a conversation with Patrick.
- Venues: check date for the named setting.
- Contact: complete the chosen contact route.
- FAQ: resolve the final objection, then act.

The physical CUE control remains globally available; page-level CTAs should respond to the page’s specific mission.

## Homepage accreditation audit

### Mission

Move a suitable couple from immediate recognition to desire, confidence, personal connection, proof, and a decisive availability action.

### Keep

- Physical performance-pad navigation.
- Physical date-check CUE control.
- Living VU meter.
- Hero documentary collage.
- “Nobody should notice the microphone.”
- “The DJ you plan with is the DJ who shows up.”
- Music-direction section with must-plays, no-go tracks, and requests.
- Real review deck.
- Final availability/consult conversion.

### Rework

- The headline “Your wedding. A variety of the best.” is understandable but broad. It should remain under copy accreditation rather than being treated as permanent merely because it fits the composition.
- The current section order does not follow the Constitution’s CH00–CH07 order. Build and reviews currently arrive before Sound Check. This may be creatively defensible, but the energy curve must be tested deliberately rather than inherited accidentally.
- The hero collage is reused in Encore. The ending should return to the machine without visually replaying Arrival.
- The video proof section needs a visible strategic role. Its accessible heading currently describes atmosphere but does not explain what the visitor should learn or believe.
- Some captions and labels still use numbered “chapter” language that the visitor does not need.

### Page decision

**KEEP / TARGETED REWORK.** The homepage is the strongest page and should not be rebuilt wholesale. It needs chapter accreditation, copy accreditation, and a deliberate energy-curve review.

Establish musical fit, explain ceremony-through-reception coverage, demonstrate that Patrick handles both sound and dance-floor decisions, and make checking the date feel logical.

### Keep

- “Wedding is the format. The music is yours.”
- Musical range and open-format explanation.
- Real wedding photograph.
- Revised value propositions: “Your taste leads,” “One soundtrack for the whole day,” and “Clear before the day.”
- Concrete ceremony, dinner, speech, and reception coverage.
- Mixer-knob planning markers.

### Rework

- “Your story, your energy, your people” is generic and could belong to many wedding vendors.
- “One cohesive sound and flow” returns to the overused flow vocabulary.
- The value cards are clearer than before but still use a repeated three-card layout.
- The page includes its own FAQ despite a dedicated FAQ page. Keep only the questions that are decisive for choosing the Weddings service.
- The planning process should demonstrate what Patrick actually does at each step, not simply rename a standard vendor process.

### Combine or remove

- Combine the broad experience copy with the planning process if both sections are answering “What is it like to work with Patrick?”
- Remove any question that does not materially affect choosing the service.

### Page decision

**REWORK.** The page has a strong core and improved copy, but it needs a more distinctive service architecture and fewer repeated propositions.

## Packages audit

### Mission

Help a couple understand scope, compare realistic options, identify the closest fit, and know what happens before committing.

### Keep

- Direct promise: “Know what you are booking before you commit.”
- Clear distinction between celebration, complete wedding, and expanded production.
- Concrete inclusion lists.
- Explanation of what happens after an inquiry.
- Personal reply, written quote, coverage, and add-on clarity.

### Rework

- The page promises clarity, but package boundaries remain soft. Hours, ceremony coverage, audio zones, lighting, and customization need a faster comparison method.
- The three equal package cards are a conventional pricing-page pattern underneath DJ styling.
- “Signature,” “bespoke,” “premium,” and “push the experience further” sound like vendor-language tiers rather than Patrick speaking.
- “Every package includes” repeats several promises already present in the packages themselves.
- The absence of starting-price context may frustrate visitors if prices cannot be published. If pricing must remain custom, explain why in one direct sentence and specify what changes the quote.

### Recommended composition

Use a real channel-strip or routing-board comparison:

- each option behaves like a channel;
- shared inclusions live on the master bus;
- differences are immediately visible;
- add-ons appear as sends or expansion modules; and
- the visitor can identify the closest channel without decoding DJ terminology.

### Page decision

**MAJOR REWORK.** Strong commercial purpose, but the presentation and tier language currently feel more conventional than the brand standard allows.

## Venue hub and venue-template audit

### Mission

Help couples planning a named Squamish or Sea-to-Sky venue understand relevant sound, logistics, and reception considerations—and establish Patrick’s useful local knowledge.

### Keep

- Named venues.
- Clear statement that the guides are planning context, not exclusivity claims.
- Practical subjects such as room layout, guest movement, weather, sound zones, ceremony placement, and load-in.
- A venue-specific availability path.

### Rework

- Never tell visitors that pages are not “SEO shells.” Demonstrate usefulness instead of defending the page’s existence.
- Many venue descriptions use interchangeable language about pacing, arcs, warmth, transitions, and energy.
- Venue pages need a higher evidence threshold before claiming familiarity.
- Repeated template sections risk making distinct venues feel identical.

### Venue evidence standard

Every venue page must clearly distinguish among:

- **Verified first-hand knowledge** — Patrick has worked there or personally assessed it.
- **Verified public planning information** — supported by the venue’s own published material.
- **General planning guidance** — useful but not represented as venue-specific experience.

Each page should contain only claims supported by one of those categories.

### Page decision

**REWORK TEMPLATE / ACCREDIT INDIVIDUALLY.** Do not treat approval of the venue template as approval of every venue page.

Answer a location-specific acquisition question without duplicating the Weddings page or creating thin SEO variants.

### Squamish page

Strongest location page because Squamish is the actual base and service focus. Keep operational local details. Combine the repeated local-value, atmosphere, venue, and related-planning sections. Replace “Atmosphere Arc · Roomflow” language with something Patrick would say.

**Decision: REWORK / REDUCE.**

### Vancouver-planning page

Useful intent: couples live in Vancouver but marry in Squamish. The distinction is commercially valid. The page repeats “local advantage” through several sections and risks sounding defensive about not being a Vancouver storefront.

Keep one strong explanation of why the event location matters, then move to service, proof, and action.

**Decision: COMBINE / REDUCE.**

### Whistler page

The current site elsewhere states that service is focused on weddings held in Squamish, while a dedicated Whistler wedding DJ page markets Whistler service. This creates a potential service-boundary contradiction that must be resolved by Patrick before copy accreditation.

If Whistler is genuinely in scope, define the conditions and use defensible venue evidence. If it is not in scope, remove or redirect the page rather than optimizing it further.

**Decision: BUSINESS CLARIFICATION REQUIRED.**

### Mission

Provide genuinely useful expertise, demonstrate Patrick’s way of thinking, earn search visibility, and give undecided couples reasons to return or inquire.

### Keep

- Real planning questions.
- Strong article subjects tied to choosing a DJ and building a dance floor.
- Sea-to-Sky context.
- Editorial separation between practical guides and emotional stories.

### Rework

- “Start here” and similar hub headings do not communicate value.
- Hub pages contain internal-link paragraphs that feel written for site architecture rather than people.

- Stories must not imply documentary proof when they are editorial composites rather than reports from a specific wedding.

**KEEP CATEGORY / REWORK HUBS AND COPY.**

## Page intensity and composition plan

The creative system should change volume according to the page’s job.

### Homepage

Full energy curve from LOW to PEAK. This remains the most expressive page.

### Weddings

MEDIUM with one high-energy release. Service mechanics and documentary wedding moments should share the page.

### Packages

LOW to MEDIUM. Clarity outranks visual attack. Hardware should organize comparison.

### Reviews

MEDIUM. Evidence, documentary fragments, attribution, and afterimage—not card-grid polish.

### About

LOW. Patrick, portrait, working detail, paper, first-person language, and controlled imperfection.

### Contact and FAQ

LOW. Functional, readable, and quiet. Expressive controls may frame the action but must never compete with it.

### Venues and local pillars

MEDIUM. Place, real photographs, route or setting context, and operational evidence.

### Guides and stories

LOW to MEDIUM. Print-led reading with occasional documentary or equipment interruption.

## Priority production roadmap

## Phase 1 — Governance and truth

1. Confirm the actual service boundary: Squamish only, broader Sea-to-Sky, or conditional Whistler/Pemberton work.
2. Establish evidence status for every venue page.
3. Freeze the section-contract and accreditation process.
4. Create a visitor-facing copy blacklist and repetition tracker from the two governing documents.

## Phase 2 — Information architecture reduction

1. Reduce Contact to its essential conversion actions.
2. Strip FAQ back to decisive questions and direct answers.
3. Remove or combine link-only and repeated reassurance sections.
4. Give every interior page one primary conversion action.

## Phase 3 — Highest-value page recomposition

1. Recompose Reviews as evidence rather than cards.
2. Rebuild Packages around a meaningful comparison system.
3. Tighten Weddings into a distinctive service page.
4. Recompose About at LOW intensity around Patrick.

## Phase 4 — Local and editorial accreditation

1. Accredit Squamish, Vancouver-planning, and Whistler pages individually.
2. Rebuild the venue template with explicit evidence categories.
3. Audit every venue page before publication.
4. Separate guides, stories, and venue content into clearly different editorial forms.

## Phase 5 — Homepage accreditation

1. Review the section order against the intended energy curve.
2. Accredit the opening headline.
3. Give the video section a clear proof job.
4. Create a distinct Encore composition rather than reusing Arrival art.
5. Complete desktop, mobile, reduced-motion, accessibility, and performance review for each chapter.

---

## Definition of Tier S completion

The site is not Tier S because every surface is highly styled. It reaches the standard when:

- every page has one clear business mission;
- every section has one necessary job;
- every important claim has proof;
- Patrick sounds like Patrick;
- machine elements explain or enable something;
- documentary wedding material carries emotional truth;
- Squamish appears through defensible specificity;
- page compositions differ according to their purpose;
- mobile is intentionally recomposed;
- accessibility and conversion remain obvious;
- no page reads like an SEO shell;
- no section is retained merely because it looks impressive; and
- Patrick has explicitly approved each production-accredited page.
