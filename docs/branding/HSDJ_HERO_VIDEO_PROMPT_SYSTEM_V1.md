# HSDJ Hero Video Prompt System V1

## Objective

Create short, silent wedding-reception clips that feel observed rather than staged. The montage should show a real crowd responding to music across visibly different venues. It must not resemble a first-dance advertisement, a choreographed music video, or glossy AI stock footage.

## Non-negotiable crowd direction

- No central subject and no featured bride or groom.
- No couple surrounded by a circle.
- No formations, line dances, choreography, or synchronized movement.
- Frame the camera from inside the crowd, with guests crossing and partially obscuring one another.
- Mix ages, heights, body shapes, faces, clothing styles, and levels of enthusiasm.
- Include ordinary late-night details: loosened ties, jackets off, wrinkled shirts, messy hair, flushed faces, perspiration, and abandoned shoes.
- Give separate groups different actions so movement feels independent rather than model-wide.
- Explicitly prohibit repeated vertical bouncing and crowd-wide pulsing.
- Describe actions with different start and stop times: a turn ends before a hand rises elsewhere; someone crosses while another guest pauses; energetic movement sits beside conversation and near-stillness.
- Prefer lateral steps, turns, pauses, gestures, awkward footwork, carrying drinks, fixing a shoe, retrieving clothing, laughing, talking, and leaving or entering the frame. Do not rely on jumping as the default signifier of energy.
- Keep venue architecture visible enough that every clip reads as a different location.
- For outdoor clips, preserve indoor-club density: the landscape should appear only in brief gaps between nearby bodies, not by pulling the camera back or thinning the crowd.
- Transfer the basement energy through proximity, laughter, shout-singing, collisions, interruptions and reactive camera movement—not by asking the whole crowd to jump harder.

## Camera and finish

- Single continuous eight-second 16:9 take.
- Default to landscape footage casually recorded by a guest on a recent iPhone using the 1x main camera.
- Keep the phone inside the room at chest or shoulder height and let the operator physically squeeze between groups.
- Use wide deep focus, ordinary phone-video motion, slight stabilization lag, mild rolling shutter, modest low-light grain, imperfect automatic white balance, exposure breathing under DJ lights, and an occasional brief autofocus shift caused by a nearby shoulder.
- Allow imperfect framing, hand corrections, foreground backs, elbows, hair, clothing, and even another guest's phone to obscure part of the image.
- Avoid cinematic shallow depth of field, dolly language, immaculate colour grading, beauty lighting, and professionally perfect composition.
- For club-night clips, describe the source as a raw personal camera-roll file rather than merely “iPhone-style.” Start late or framed too low, let the operator be bumped, and allow the stabilizer to catch up after a correction.
- In dark scenes, permit crushed noisy shadows, clipped DJ-light highlights, red-blue chroma noise, exposure pumping, colour-temperature hunting, LED streaks, compression texture, mild oversharpening, lens-smudge flare, and temporary foreground focus errors.
- Apply a restrained local camera-roll treatment after export when Flow still produces an immaculate image. Preserve faces and motion while adding low-light noise, less forgiving contrast, highlight clipping and consumer compression.
- No dialogue is required; final selected clips will be delivered without audio.

## Negative constraints

No posing, glamour-ad lighting, perfect smiles, plastic skin, cloned guests, repeated faces, frozen spectators, fused hands, extra fingers or limbs, warped anatomy, melting faces, flicker, slow motion, whip pans, cuts, readable signs, logos, captions, or text.

## Venue sequence

1. Mountain timber-and-glass reception.
2. Converted industrial brick loft.
3. Outdoor sailcloth tent in a forest clearing.
4. Contemporary hotel ballroom with a low ceiling and architectural lighting.
5. Rustic coastal hall with timber beams and open doors to the night.
6. Intimate restaurant reception with a compressed dance floor.
7. DJ-booth-side crowd perspective.
8. Peak-energy closing shot with the whole room moving.

## Movement concepts to rotate

- Loose Latin footwork that breaks down into laughter rather than a polished routine.
- An older relative dancing minimally beside younger guests moving more energetically.
- One comic low move or air-guitar gesture that nearby friends react to without copying.
- A guest crossing with water, shoes, or a jacket while the dance floor continues behind them.
- A single spin, followed later by one raised hand elsewhere in the room.
- Friends shout-singing, talking closely, pointing, laughing, pausing, and rejoining.
- Guests entering or squeezing between clusters, creating useful foreground occlusion.
- A hot, compressed club-night floor where the camera is physically jostled and people pass within inches of the phone.

No single concept should appear in every clip. Each venue receives a different combination and a different camera path.

## Deliberate singalong-huddle exception

A single montage clip may intentionally use a tight circle, linked shoulders and shared jumping when the emotional event is friends spontaneously belting a chorus together. This is an exception, not the default crowd grammar. Keep it human by specifying staggered takeoffs and landings, different jump heights, sideways drift, slipping and reconnecting arms, missed lyrics, laughter, breath pauses and a circle that continually changes shape. Background guests must continue moving independently. Avoid perfect circular geometry, uniform shoulder motion, repeated loops and choreography.
