# HSDJ page purpose and content ownership

Every public page gets one primary job. Supporting links may point to another page's subject, but they should not repeat that page's explanation.

## Primary commercial pages

- Home: create recognition and desire. Show the full-day musical arc, real atmosphere, Patrick's presence, and one route into availability.
- Weddings: explain what Patrick handles across the wedding day. Own ceremony, microphones, transitions, dinner, speeches, and dance-floor coverage.
- Packages: help a couple choose the right scope. Own Complete Wedding, A Great Party, common inclusions, useful additions, and the booking sequence.
- About: establish personal trust. Own Patrick's background, temperament, working style, and the fact that the person couples plan with is the person who arrives.
- Reviews: prove the promise using client language. Do not paraphrase the testimonials into another sales page.
- FAQ: answer practical objections and edge cases. Keep answers direct; link out instead of retelling Weddings, Packages, or About.
- Contact: check availability and start a conversation. Do not add another biography, package explanation, or sales maze.

## Location pages

- Squamish: own local operational knowledge and the Squamish venue corridor.
- Whistler: retired as a standalone page. It redirects to the Squamish service page rather than duplicating location copy for an out-of-area market.
- Vancouver planning page: own remote planning for couples who live in Vancouver and marry in Squamish. It is not a second Squamish service overview.
- Venue hub: help visitors choose a setting and reach the relevant venue guide.
- Venue detail: own the physical and logistical questions created by that individual setting.

## Editorial pages

- Guides hub: route visitors to useful planning instruction.
- Individual guide: answer one planning question thoroughly and practically.
- Stories hub: route visitors to atmosphere-led editorial pieces.
- Individual story: make one place or dance-floor condition feel tangible. It must not pretend to be a client recap when it is editorial observation.

## Duplication rules

- The full date-check and consult sequence belongs on Packages and Contact. Elsewhere, use a short contextual invitation only when conversion is a natural next step.
- Package definitions belong on Packages.
- Full wedding-day coverage belongs on Weddings.
- Patrick's biography and working philosophy belong on About.
- Testimonial interpretation should be minimal on Reviews; the clients' words are the proof.
- “45 minutes / No pressure / Just clarity” is not a site-wide refrain.
- Editorial hubs do not use the full stereo conversion deck.
- Location pages should describe what changes in that location, not repeat the general service promise.
