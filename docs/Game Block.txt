PRODUCT THESIS
================================================================================

A visitor checking wedding-date availability is at one of the highest-intent
moments on the Howe Sound Wedding DJ website.

The current success experience answers:

"Is Patrick available?"

But some visitors may then immediately continue comparing other DJs without
booking a consultation.

We want to create an optional, surprising, genuinely fun micro-experience that:

1. rewards the visitor's curiosity;
2. keeps them engaged with Howe Sound DJ longer;
3. demonstrates the playful/music-first personality of the brand;
4. makes the website memorable;
5. differentiates Howe Sound DJ from conventional wedding-DJ websites;
6. creates a natural emotional bridge from:

   "Patrick is available"

   to:

   "This guy/site is fun and different."

   to:

   "We should actually talk to Patrick."

The experience MUST NOT rely on:

- fake scarcity;
- fake competition;
- monetary bribery;
- deceptive "winning";
- fake prizes;
- forced interaction;
- conversion hostage-taking;
- casino mechanics;
- manipulative variable rewards;
- fabricated social proof;
- dark patterns.

================================================================================
WORKING CONCEPT — LOCKED
================================================================================

Canonical concept:

DO NOT PRESS → DROP!

The interaction has TWO primary stages.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STAGE 1 — DO NOT PRESS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After a visitor receives a truthful AVAILABLE result, the normal Availability
Success experience remains intact.

An optional, visually irresistible DJ-controller-style performance pad appears.

Initial label:

DO NOT
PRESS

There should be little or no explanation.

The curiosity itself is the invitation.

The visitor may:

- ignore it completely;
- continue normally to the consultation CTA;
- press it.

The game MUST NOT be required to reach the booking CTA.

When pressed:

the success experience "wakes up."

Possible bounded effects:

- performance pad illumination;
- VU meters wake;
- waveform/display activates;
- crowd silhouettes become visible/alive;
- controller LEDs activate;
- subtle deck/record movement;
- optional tiny audio build becomes available after user interaction.

Then reveal concise copy such as:

OH, YOU PRESSED IT.

Think you can nail the drop?

The same primary hardware control should transition to:

PLAY

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STAGE 2 — DROP!
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Visitor presses PLAY.

A short 3–2–1 / build sequence begins.

A lightweight waveform / timing visualization moves toward a drop.

The primary control becomes:

DROP

The visitor taps/clicks/presses the control when they believe the musical drop
will occur.

Desktop should also support an intuitive keyboard interaction such as Space
while the game itself has focus.

The game measures timing error relative to the target.

Immediate feedback occurs in this order:

1. visual/audio crowd reaction;
2. VU-meter / controller response;
3. short score/reaction copy.

The visitor may immediately:

TRY AGAIN

or continue to the existing consultation path through copy conceptually like:

LET'S TALK ABOUT YOUR ACTUAL DANCE FLOOR

The exact rendered CTA wording may be refined during implementation if required
for clarity and brand fit, but MUST preserve the existing scheduling authority
and MUST NOT create a second Calendly destination.
